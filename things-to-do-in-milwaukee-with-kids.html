Milwaukee, Wisconsin · Field Guide No. 07

Milwaukee with kids, past the zoo gates

Most families come for the County Zoo. What surprises them is how much sits around it: a handful of strong kids' museums, a real Lake Michigan beach, and a downtown that handles kids better than you'd expect.

The short answerTL;DR

Two days is about right for Milwaukee with kids. Give the first morning to the County Zoo and the afternoon to a hands-on museum like Discovery World or Betty Brinn. The second day is for the lakefront and Bradford Beach. May through September is best for the beach and the festivals. Come off-season and you lean on the indoor stops, but the city still works.

Ask most families what to do in Milwaukee and you'll hear "the zoo." It's a good answer. The County Zoo is one of the better mid-size zoos anywhere, and a full morning on its own. But it's the start of a trip here, not the whole thing. Around it sits a cluster of kids' museums, a lakefront you can walk with a stroller, and a beach that actually gets used in summer.

A first family trip usually looks like this. Spend the first morning at the zoo, since that's what the kids are picturing. Fill the afternoon with a museum that fits their age. Day two goes to the lake: the art museum, the beach, the parks along the water. What follows is how to choose between the big stops, and the small logistics that end up shaping the day.

The anchor · Book the morningMilwaukee County Zoo

Start here, and go early. (We have a full family guide to the zoo if you want the deep version.) A zoo doesn't sound like a rainy-day plan, does it? This one is. It sits eight miles west of downtown off I-94, on 190 wooded acres that fill a morning easily, and enough of it is indoor animal buildings that a grey forecast barely dents it. Open year-round, too, closed only on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day.

The primate house on a rainy morning

Two things to know before you buy. The zoo uses flex pricing, so the number moves with the day: at the gate, adults run about $12.75 to $19.75 and kids 3 to 12 about $9.75 to $16.75. Children 2 and under are free. Booking online knocks off roughly $3 a ticket. And parking is a separate $15 per car, so build that in.

From our family · travelling with a one-year-old

We travel with a one-year-old, so at any zoo we're really checking two things first: is there shade, and how far will the stroller actually go before it becomes dead weight. (A changing table is the third, once you've done a few of these.) Milwaukee holds up. The paths are wide and paved, there's real tree cover, and the animal buildings buy you a climate-controlled break when the baby's had enough. We'd still bring the carrier for inside the buildings, where a stroller is just something to park.

Field note

The zoo runs six Family Free Days in 2026 when general admission is free for everyone, you still pay parking and ride fees. Milwaukee County residents also get reduced admission on Wednesdays and a $4.14 rate on 414 Day (April 14). If your dates are flexible, a free Saturday is the single biggest money-saver here.

The rides and animal-contact areas cost extra, mostly $3 to $5 a person plus tax, carousel and little train included. Honestly? You can skip most of them and not miss a thing. The one we'd actually pay for is the train, late in the day, when a toddler has decided walking is over. One timing catch worth knowing: admission stops 45 minutes before the zoo closes, and the animal buildings shut 15 minutes before that. Cut it too fine and you'll be rushed.

Milwaukee Art Museum

The Calatrava wings, mid-open

An art museum, with a one-year-old? You'd think not. Make the exception here anyway. The Quadracci Pavilion, Santiago Calatrava's white winged hall on the lakefront, unfolds a huge sunshade twice a day like something breathing. Kids stop and stare, which is not a sentence you write about most art museums. Admission is free for children under 12, so treat it as a 30-minute stop between the beach and lunch and leave when the baby's done.

Milwaukee hands you a great zoo on arrival. The city is what you find in the afternoons.

Day two · The waterThe lakefront and Bradford Beach

Bradford Beach on a July afternoon

Milwaukee is a lake city, and day two belongs to the water. Bradford Beach is a real Lake Michigan swimming beach, summer lifeguards and all, with enough sand for a full afternoon. A proper city beach you'd actually swim at? Most places this size can't offer one. From there the lakefront path runs south past a string of parks, the art museum and Discovery World, close enough to walk or push a stroller the whole way.

Field note

Lake Michigan is cold well into summer and beach conditions change with the wind, check the daily flag before letting kids in past their knees. Mornings are calmest; afternoons bring the crowds and the breeze off the water.

Getting around, parking and food

Frozen custard in the Third Ward

Milwaukee is an easy place to drive. The stops you'll want fall into two groups: the zoo out west off I-94, and the lakefront cluster downtown. Stay near the lake and you can walk that second group. You'll drive to the zoo either way. For food, the lakefront and the Historic Third Ward have the most casual, stroller-friendly spots, and it's worth working a Milwaukee frozen custard into one afternoon. The custard is not optional, locally speaking.

Before you go

How many days do you need in Milwaukee with kids?

Two days covers it comfortably. Day one is the zoo plus an afternoon museum. Day two is the lakefront, Bradford Beach and the art museum, with time to spare. Add a third day only if you want to slow down, or tack on Lake Geneva or a Door County leg.

When is the Milwaukee County Zoo free in 2026?

The zoo offers six Family Free Days in 2026 when general admission is free for everyone, you still pay for parking and any rides. Milwaukee County residents also get reduced admission on Wednesdays and a $4.14 rate on 414 Day (April 14). Free-day admission can't be combined with online discounts.

Milwaukee County Zoo or Discovery World, which first?

Do both in one day if you can: zoo in the morning, Discovery World after lunch. The zoo rewards an early start with easier parking and more active animals, and Discovery World's indoor exhibits suit kids who are flagging by midday. Forced to pick one, go by age. The zoo works for everyone; Discovery World is at its best for 4 to 12.

How much does the Milwaukee County Zoo cost?

The zoo uses flex pricing. At the gate, adults run about $12.75–$19.75 and children 3–12 about $9.75–$16.75, with kids 2 and under free. Buying online saves roughly $3 per ticket, and parking is $15 per car on top. Confirm current rates on the zoo's site before you go.

Is downtown Milwaukee good for young kids?

More than you'd think. The lakefront group (art museum, Discovery World, Bradford Beach and the parks between them) is walkable and easy with a stroller, and the Historic Third Ward has casual food nearby. You'll drive out to the zoo, but the rest of a family day stays close together.
