Traverse City with kids, dunes, bay and cherries

Traverse City is northern Michigan's easy family base: a bay full of swimmable beaches, the giant sand dunes of Sleeping Bear a half hour away, a Great Wolf Lodge for a rainy day, and cherry orchards everywhere in summer. Here is how to shape a first family long weekend up north.

The short answerTL;DR

Give Traverse City three or four days with kids. The anchor is Sleeping Bear Dunes, about 30 to 40 minutes west, where the Dune Climb is a highlight for every age, backed by the warm, shallow Grand Traverse Bay beaches right in town. Add Great Wolf Lodge for a guaranteed rainy-day win, and in July the cherry orchards and the National Cherry Festival. Come late June through August for swimming and full season; September adds fall color and thinner crowds. It is about four hours up I-75 and US-131 from Detroit.

Traverse City is where a lot of Michigan families go to feel like they are properly up north without a brutal drive. It sits at the foot of Grand Traverse Bay, a sheltered arm of Lake Michigan with beaches warm enough to actually swim in, and it is the gateway to one of the Midwest's great natural wonders, the towering dunes of Sleeping Bear. Add cherry country, a walkable downtown and a big indoor waterpark for the off day, and you have a family base that works rain or shine. Here is how to plan it.

From Detroit, Traverse City is about four hours up I-75 and US-131, which makes it a long weekend rather than a day trip (we map it in our Detroit to Traverse City guide). See where it sits among the best weekend getaways from Detroit.

The anchor · give it a daySleeping Bear Dunes

The one unmissable outing is Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore, about 30 to 40 minutes west of Traverse City, where enormous sand dunes rise hundreds of feet straight out of Lake Michigan. The headline for kids is the Dune Climb, a giant wall of sand you scramble up and tumble down, as much fun as any playground. The Pierce Stocking Scenic Drive gives you the famous overlook without the hike, and there are gentle trails and beaches along the shore. One honest warning for the ambitious: the climb to the Lake Michigan overlook and back is a strenuous, deep-sand slog that is longer than it looks, so turn around with young kids before anyone melts down.

The easy win · right in townThe Grand Traverse Bay beaches

What makes Traverse City so easy with kids is that the beaches are right there. Grand Traverse Bay is shallow and sheltered, so the water warms up and stays calm, far gentler than the open Great Lakes shore. Clinch Park downtown has a swimming beach and a splash feature within walking distance of the main street, and Bryant Park and the West Grand Traverse Bay beaches give you more room to spread out. For young kids who just want to paddle and dig, this is the heart of the trip, and it costs nothing.

The rainy-day insuranceGreat Wolf Lodge

Great Wolf Lodge Traverse City is the family card up north's sleeve: a 38,000-square-foot indoor water park kept at 84 degrees, with slides, a wave pool and a big toddler area, attached to a themed resort with suites, an arcade and daily kids' activities. The one thing to know, as with all Great Wolf locations: the water park is for hotel guests only, so it is an overnight, not a day pass. It is the obvious move for a cold or rainy day, or simply a night the kids will rate as the best part of the trip. We cover it alongside the rest in our indoor water parks near Detroit guide.

Summer only · JulyCherry country

Traverse City calls itself the Cherry Capital of the World, and in July the orchards on the Old Mission and Leelanau peninsulas open for the season, many with u-pick, farm stands, pie and cider. The National Cherry Festival in early July brings a week of parades, air shows and midway rides to the waterfront, great fun but also the busiest and priciest week of the year, so book well ahead or dodge it on purpose. Outside festival week, a morning picking cherries and eating pie is a lovely, low-key slice of the region.

Dunes in the morning, bay beach in the afternoon. Traverse City makes it easy.

Getting the shape rightHow long, and where to base

Three to four days is right: a day at Sleeping Bear, a couple of beach days on the bay, a Great Wolf night, and time for cherries or downtown. Base yourself in or near Traverse City for the beaches and restaurants, or out toward Sleeping Bear if dunes and quiet are the priority. Summer lodging books up, especially around Cherry Festival, so reserve early. Downtown Front Street covers rainy-afternoon browsing, ice cream and family dinners, and the TART trail is an easy flat bike path along the water.

Before you go

Is Traverse City good for kids?

Yes, it is one of the easiest family bases up north. The Grand Traverse Bay beaches in town are warm and shallow, Sleeping Bear Dunes is a half hour away, Great Wolf Lodge covers rainy days, and cherry season adds u-pick orchards in July. It suits all ages, from toddlers paddling at Clinch Park to older kids tackling the Dune Climb.

How many days do you need in Traverse City with kids?

Three to four days is the sweet spot, with a day at Sleeping Bear Dunes, a couple of beach days on the bay, a Great Wolf night, and time for cherries or downtown. Two days works if you stay tight, but the drive up from Detroit (about four hours) makes a longer stay worthwhile.

What is there to do in Traverse City with kids?

Climb Sleeping Bear Dunes, swim the warm shallow bay beaches (Clinch Park is walkable from downtown), spend a night at Great Wolf Lodge, pick cherries in July, bike the flat TART trail, and browse Front Street for ice cream and dinner. It mixes big nature with easy in-town wins, rain or shine.

When is the best time to visit Traverse City with kids?

Late June through August for swimming, the dunes and full season, with July adding cherry picking and the National Cherry Festival (which is fun but very busy). September brings fall color and thinner crowds, though the water cools. Great Wolf Lodge works year-round for an indoor waterpark fix.

Is the Sleeping Bear Dune Climb hard with kids?

The main Dune Climb wall is pure fun for all ages, scrambling up and rolling down the sand. But the full hike from the top over the dunes to the Lake Michigan overlook and back is a strenuous, deep-sand trek much longer than it looks, so with young kids, enjoy the climb itself and turn back before anyone overheats.
