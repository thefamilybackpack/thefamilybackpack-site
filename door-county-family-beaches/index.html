Door County, Wisconsin · Beaches

The best family beaches in Door County

Door County has water on both sides, and that decides everything. The Green Bay side is calm, shallow and warms up early, ideal for little kids. The Lake Michigan side has the big sand and the big waves. Here's where to swim, by age.

The short answerTL;DR

For young kids, stick to the Green Bay side: Nicolet Beach (in Peninsula State Park) and Sister Bay Beach are shallow, sandy and warm up early, with playgrounds right there. For the classic big-sand, big-wave beach day, cross to the Lake Michigan side and Whitefish Dunes. State-park beaches need a Wisconsin sticker; village beaches are free. There are no lifeguards anywhere, so the calm bay side is the safer bet with little ones.

The first thing to understand about Door County beaches is which water you're on. The bay side (Green Bay, to the west) is protected, shallow and warms up weeks earlier, so it's where you want to be with toddlers. The lake side (Lake Michigan, to the east) has the dramatic dunes and the real waves, which is wonderful for older kids and less so for a two-year-old. Pick your side by your youngest child, and the rest sorts itself out.

The bay side · Best for little kidsCalm, shallow, warm

Nicolet Beach, inside Peninsula State Park near Fish Creek, is the most family-ready beach in the county: a sandy Green Bay beach with genuinely shallow water, a playground right behind it, a snack bar, restrooms, sand volleyball and kayak, SUP and hydrobike rentals ten feet from the water. There's even a free beach wheelchair. It warms earlier than the lake and has buoyed no-wake zones keeping boats away from swimmers. It needs a Wisconsin State Park sticker ($10 day / $28 annual) and the lot fills fast on July weekends, so arrive early.

Sister Bay Beach is the other easy win, and it's free. At about 1,900 feet it's Door County's largest beachfront park, with a swim pier, a floating swim platform, a kayak launch and what Destination Door County calls the best playground in the county. The sand is a touch rockier than Nicolet's, but the water is just as calm and warm, and the setting, right in Sister Bay's Waterfront Park with restaurants a short walk away, is hard to beat for a family afternoon.

From our family · travelling with a one-year-old

With a one-year-old, we don't overthink beaches: we want shallow, warm and a playground for when the sand gets old in twenty minutes. The bay side delivers all three, and Nicolet in particular meant we could rent a paddleboard while the baby splashed at the edge. We skip the Lake Michigan beaches with a baby, the waves and the cold water aren't worth it yet.

The lake side · Big sand, big wavesFor older kids and sandcastles

When the kids are old enough for waves, cross to Whitefish Dunes State Park near Sturgeon Bay. It's a mile of Lake Michigan sand backed by Wisconsin's tallest dunes (up to 93 feet), with that proper big-lake feel, waves and all, though the water stays only about waist-deep a hundred feet out. There's a boardwalk, a nature center, a playground and volleyball, and three beach-access points, one of them ADA-accessible. It needs a state-park sticker. Just up the shore, Cave Point County Park (dramatic bluffs and clear water) is a stunning stop, but it's for looking, not swimming with little kids.

Field note

There are no lifeguards at any Door County beach, bay or lake. On the Lake Michigan side, watch for wind and wave changes and keep little kids close; the bay side is far more forgiving. Whichever beach, check the daily conditions before letting kids in past their knees.

Two more, if the big beaches are full

Anclam Beach in Baileys Harbor has a playground and a jetty that shelters the swimming area from Lake Michigan swells, a nice middle ground. Otumba Park in Sturgeon Bay is a quieter local pick with a shallow, sandy entry and a big wooden playground, handy if you're based in the gateway city or the state-park lots have filled.

Before you go

Which Door County beach is best for toddlers?

Nicolet Beach in Peninsula State Park, or Sister Bay Beach. Both are on the calm, shallow Green Bay side, warm up earlier than Lake Michigan, and have playgrounds right behind the sand. Sister Bay is free; Nicolet needs a Wisconsin State Park sticker.

Which Door County beaches have the warmest water?

The Green Bay side beaches, mainly Nicolet Beach and Sister Bay Beach. Because they sit on shallow, protected bays, the sun warms the water much faster than the deep, open Lake Michigan side. The lake-side beaches like Whitefish Dunes stay cooler well into summer.

Do Door County beaches have lifeguards?

No. None of Door County's beaches are lifeguarded, on either the bay or the lake side. The calm, shallow bay beaches (Nicolet, Sister Bay) are the safer choice with young kids; on the Lake Michigan side, watch the wind and waves and keep little ones close.

Do you need a pass for Door County beaches?

It depends. State-park beaches like Nicolet Beach (Peninsula State Park) and Whitefish Dunes need a Wisconsin State Park vehicle sticker ($10 day / $28 annual). Village beaches in Sister Bay, Ephraim and Fish Creek are free to enter, though parking fills up fast on summer weekends.
