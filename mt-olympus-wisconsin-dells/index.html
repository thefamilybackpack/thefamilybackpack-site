Mt. Olympus, with kids

Mt. Olympus is the Dells' confusing giant: four parks in one, water and theme, indoor and outdoor, with go-karts, roller coasters and the tallest waterslide in America. It can be a brilliant-value family trip or an overwhelming one. Here is how it actually works, the ride heights, and whether it is worth it.

Mt. Olympus is the most misunderstood attraction in the Wisconsin Dells, and the confusion costs families money and sanity. It is not one park, it is four: an outdoor water park, an outdoor theme park with real roller coasters, an indoor water park, and an indoor theme park, all Greek-themed, all on one admission, spread across about 150 acres. That combination is its superpower (a rainy day just moves indoors) and its trap (it is huge, and the little-kid areas are far from the entrance). Understand how it works before you go, and it can be one of the best-value family trips in the Dells.

Field note

The money move that changes everything: if you stay at a Mt. Olympus hotel, the whole family gets park tickets included, water parks and theme parks both. That is the park's core deal and almost always cheaper than buying day admission, which is sold through rotating packages and promotions rather than a clear gate price. If you are coming as a day visitor, verify the current price and any parking fee directly before you go, there are recent reports of a parking charge where parking used to be free.

The thing that decides your dayMt. Olympus rides by height

With coasters and go-karts on top of the water rides, height matters even more here than at a plain waterpark. Here is the picture for the headline attractions. The thrill coasters and the tallest slides sit at 48 inches and up, a moderate tier opens at 42 inches, and the go-karts have their own graduated rules, little ones ride along, bigger kids drive. Always confirm on site.

Field note

The toddler reality nobody tells you: Mt. Olympus's kids' areas are at the back of the property, a genuinely long walk from the entrance with little legs and a stroller. The payoff is real once you get there, the outdoor Lost City of Atlantis is a three-acre water playground with a dump bucket and kiddie slides (42 inches for the slides), and indoors there are gentle kiddie slides and a splash area. The practical fix is to stay at a Mt. Olympus hotel, which puts you closer and lets you nap-break back at the room, a big deal with under-fives here.

How it worksFour parks, indoor and outdoor

The four-park setup is the whole story. The outdoor water park (Poseidon's Rage wave pool, the Rise of Icarus, family slides) and the outdoor theme park (the Hades 360, Zeus, Cyclops and Pegasus wood coasters, plus nine go-kart tracks) run seasonally, roughly May to October. The indoor water park (Medusa's Slidewheel, Hydra, kiddie areas) runs year-round, which is what makes Mt. Olympus a cold-weather option too. The indoor theme park generally runs the opposite season to the outdoor one (confirm current hours). One standard ticket covers all four, though a few extras like the Skycoaster carry an added cost.

The honest questionIs Mt. Olympus worth it, and one day or two?

Worth it for whom? Mt. Olympus is at its best for families with thrill-seeking older kids and teens, who can ride the coasters, the go-karts and the big slides and genuinely get a theme-park-plus-waterpark day for one price, especially as hotel guests. For families built around toddlers and little kids, it is a weaker fit than a dedicated kiddie-friendly resort: the best kids' areas are far from the gate and the headline rides are off-limits. On one day or two: a day visitor can cover the highlights in one long day, but because a hotel stay bundles the tickets and the four parks are so spread out, families often make it a two-day resort trip and lean indoors if the weather turns.

From our family · making Mt. Olympus work

Our lesson at Mt. Olympus: stay on property, or it gets expensive and exhausting fast. As hotel guests the tickets were free and we could walk back for a toddler nap instead of hiking to the car. With a teen and a little one, we divided the day, the teen chased coasters and the Rise of Icarus, we camped at Lost City of Atlantis with the small one, and we all met at the wave pool. If your kids are all little, we would honestly point you at a more toddler-focused Dells resort first; this park rewards height.

Before you go

Is Mt. Olympus worth it?

For families with thrill-seeking older kids and teens, yes, especially as hotel guests, since one ticket covers four parks (water and theme, indoor and outdoor) with coasters, go-karts and the tallest waterslide in America. For families built around toddlers, it is a weaker fit, because the best kiddie areas are far from the entrance and the headline rides require 48 inches.

Do you have to stay at the hotel to go to Mt. Olympus?

No, but staying at a Mt. Olympus hotel is the best value because guests get park tickets included for the whole family. Day visitors can buy admission, but it is sold through packages and promotions rather than a simple gate price, so it is worth comparing against a hotel package. Verify current day pricing directly with the park.

How tall do you have to be for the rides at Mt. Olympus?

The thrill coasters (Hades 360, Zeus, Cyclops) and the Rise of Icarus waterslide require 48 inches, and the indoor Hydra body slide needs 55 inches. A moderate tier (Pegasus, Medusa's Slidewheel, the Lost City of Atlantis slides) opens at 42 inches. Go-karts are graduated, so little kids ride along from 36 inches, and solo driving is around 55 inches.

Is parking free at Mt. Olympus?

Historically parking was free, but there are recent visitor reports of a parking fee, and the park does not clearly publish the current charge. Confirm whether there is a parking fee, and how much, directly with Mt. Olympus before you go, so it does not catch you out.

Is Mt. Olympus open in winter?

The indoor water park runs year-round, so Mt. Olympus is a winter option. The outdoor water park and the outdoor theme park (coasters and go-karts) are seasonal, roughly May to October. The indoor theme park generally runs the opposite season to the outdoor one, so confirm current hours for your dates.

Is Mt. Olympus good for toddlers?

It is a weaker fit for toddlers than some Dells resorts. The kiddie areas (the outdoor Lost City of Atlantis and indoor kiddie slides) are good, but they sit at the back of a large property, a long walk with little legs, and the headline rides need 48 inches. Staying at a Mt. Olympus hotel helps by putting you closer and allowing nap breaks.
