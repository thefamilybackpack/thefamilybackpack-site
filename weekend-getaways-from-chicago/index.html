Weekend getaways from Chicago, with the kids

Chicago is one of the best launch pads in the country for a family weekend, with lakeshore towns, waterpark country and a couple of great small cities all within a tank of gas. The trick is being honest about drive time: a true two-day weekend and a three-day long weekend are different trips. Here is where to go, sorted by how far it really is, and who each one suits.

The open road north out of Chicago
The short answerTL;DR

For a true two-day weekend, stay under about three and a half hours: Milwaukee (around 90 minutes, the easiest win) and the Wisconsin Dells (about 3 hours, the waterpark classic) are the two strongest family picks. For a three-day long weekend, the payoff gets bigger: Door County for the shoreline, and St. Louis or Louisville for a city with a lot of free, kid-friendly things to do. Closer in, Lake Geneva, Galena and Starved Rock are the classic short-hop escapes. Match the trip to the calendar: do not try to cram a four-hour-each-way drive into a Saturday-Sunday.

Living in or near Chicago is a quiet superpower for family travel. Point the car north and you hit lake towns, a lazy-river hotel belt and Wisconsin's whole vacation country; point it south or east and a couple of genuinely fun, affordable small cities are within range. The only thing that trips families up is drive time. A getaway that looks close on the map can eat half your Saturday and half your Sunday in the car, which is miserable with kids. So this guide is organized the way you should actually decide: by how far each place really is, split into easy two-day weekends and longer three-day trips, with an honest word on who each one suits.

The two-day weekendsUnder three and a half hours, home by Sunday night

These are the trips you can leave for after work or on a Saturday morning and still feel like you had two real days, because the drive is short enough not to dominate the weekend. For families around Chicago, this band is where most getaways should live.

The easiest win · about 90 minutesMilwaukee, Wisconsin

If you want the lowest-effort, highest-reward family weekend from Chicago, it is Milwaukee, barely 90 minutes up I-94. It is a proper small city with an outsized amount for kids: a world-class zoo, a hands-on children's museum (Betty Brinn), the Milwaukee Public Museum, a free art museum with a dramatic moving roof, and an easy, walkable lakefront. Because the drive is so short, you can arrive by late morning and get a full day in before you have even unpacked. It works in any season, which makes it the reliable default when you just need to get out of town. See our full Milwaukee with kids guide, where to stay with a pool, and the best museums for kids.

The lake-and-canyon close-insLake Geneva, Starved Rock and Galena

Three more short hops round out the easy end. Lake Geneva, Wisconsin (about 90 minutes) is the classic quick lake escape: a walkable resort town on a pretty lake, with a famous shore path, boat tours, beaches in summer and a big indoor-waterpark resort for the cold months. Starved Rock State Park, Illinois (about 90 minutes southwest) is the outdoor option, a river gorge laced with canyons and waterfall trails that are a genuine adventure for kids who can walk a few miles, with a historic lodge to stay in. Galena, Illinois (about two and three-quarter hours) is a preserved 19th-century main street in the hilly northwest corner, with family resorts, a small ski-and-tube hill in winter and an easy-going, old-timey feel. And an hour east, the Indiana Dunes pair a Lake Michigan beach with big climbable sand dunes, the closest beach-and-dunes day there is.

The waterpark classic · about 3 hoursWisconsin Dells, Wisconsin

The Wisconsin Dells is the quintessential Midwest family weekend, and from Chicago it is a very doable three-hour drive straight up I-90. It calls itself the Waterpark Capital of the World, and the formula is simple: you book a waterpark resort, put on wristbands, and the hotel becomes the vacation. The indoor parks are warm year-round, so this is as much a February trip as a July one, and there is plenty beyond the water, the Original Wisconsin Ducks amphibious tours, a deer park, go-karts and mini-golf. It is the strongest two-to-three-day pick in range if you have younger kids who just want to swim. Start with our Wisconsin Dells with kids guide and our breakdown of the best Dells waterpark resorts, and if the dates are flexible, our guide to the best time to visit.

Field note

The drive-time honesty test: a destination is a true two-day weekend only if the one-way drive is short enough that you still get a full day on both Saturday and Sunday. Around three and a half hours is the practical ceiling for that with kids, because beyond it you lose most of one day to the road each way. Anything farther is a three-day trip, and treating it as a two-day one is how families end up spending a "weekend away" mostly in the car. When in doubt, add an hour to the map estimate for stops, traffic leaving the city, and the reality of travelling with children.

The three-day long weekendsFour to five hours, worth it with an extra day

These destinations are too far for a tight Saturday-to-Sunday, but with a Friday or a Monday added they open up, and the payoff is a bigger change of scene. Build in the extra day and the drive stops feeling like a tax.

The shoreline · about 4 to 5 hoursDoor County, Wisconsin

Door County, the thumb of land poking into Lake Michigan north of Green Bay, is the Midwest's answer to a New England coast, and it is a long-weekend favorite for a reason. You get calm, shallow bay beaches on one side, rugged Lake Michigan shore on the other, a string of small harbor towns, cherry orchards, lighthouses and state parks. It is farther than it looks (figure four to five hours depending on which end you aim for), so it earns three days rather than two. For summer it is one of the best family trips in the region. Dig into our Door County with kids guide, the best family beaches, a ready-made family itinerary, and where to stay.

The free-city value pick · about 4.5 hoursSt. Louis, Missouri

For a city long weekend with a genuinely low price tag, St. Louis is hard to beat, about four and a half hours south on I-55. Its signature feature for families is how much is free: the excellent Saint Louis Zoo, the Science Center, the Art Museum and more charge no admission, which takes the sting out of a multi-day trip. Add the Gateway Arch with its tram to the top, the gloriously strange City Museum (a giant indoor-outdoor playscape built from salvage), and Grant's Farm, and you have easily three days of kid-friendly things to do. See our St. Louis with kids guide, the wonderfully odd City Museum, and where to stay.

The quirky-and-affordable pick · about 5 hoursLouisville, Kentucky

Louisville is the sleeper of the group, roughly a five-hour drive southeast, and a brilliant value for a long weekend. It is packed with hands-on, slightly offbeat attractions kids love: the Louisville Mega Cavern (an underground tram, ropes course and zip lines in a former mine), the Louisville Slugger Museum with its giant bat and factory tour, the Kentucky Science Center, a strong zoo and the Muhammad Ali Center. It rarely feels crowded and prices are gentle. Plan it with our Louisville with kids guide, the Mega Cavern, the Slugger Museum, and where to stay.

Seasonal specialFrankenmuth, Michigan, for a Christmas weekend

One more worth flagging for the right time of year: Frankenmuth, Michigan, a Bavarian-themed Christmas town about four and a half hours east. It is a very specific long-weekend trip, built around the world's largest Christmas store, a decorated village, family-style chicken dinners and indoor-waterpark hotels, and it is magic in November and December. Out of season it is quieter and less worth the drive. If you are reading this in the fall, see our Frankenmuth at Christmas guide.

Choosing wellHow to pick the right one

A quick way to decide. If you have two days and young kids who mostly want to swim, it is the Dells; if you want the least driving for the most city, it is Milwaukee; if you want a close-in lake or an outdoor adventure, it is Lake Geneva or Starved Rock. If you have three days, go bigger: Door County for summer shoreline, St. Louis for a free-leaning city trip, Louisville for something a bit different and cheap. And match it to the season: waterparks and cities work year-round, the shoreline and the parks are summer trips, and in the cold months the move is a winter getaway built around warm water or a Christmas town. From our family · weekends out of Chicago

The mistake we made early on was being too ambitious with distance. We tried to do a five-hour-each-way place on a normal weekend, and the kids spent more time in car seats than anywhere else, which nobody enjoyed. Now the rule is simple: for a two-day weekend we stay inside about three hours, and we save the farther spots for the long weekends when we have a third day. The closer trips are not a consolation prize either. A short drive to Milwaukee or the Dells, with the day starting before lunch instead of at dinnertime, often makes for a better weekend than a glamorous place that swallows the driving days.

Chicago weekend-getaway planning checklist
  • Decide first whether it is a 2-day or a 3-day trip; that sets the distance
  • Keep one-way drives under about 3.5 hours for a true two-day weekend
  • Add an hour to map estimates for traffic out of the city, stops and kids
  • Book waterpark resorts and summer lodging early; weekends sell out
  • Match the destination to the season (shoreline in summer, waterparks year-round)
  • Leave early to get a full first day, rather than arriving at dinnertime

Before you go

What is the best weekend getaway from Chicago with kids?

For a true two-day weekend, Milwaukee (about 90 minutes) is the easiest win, with a great zoo, children's museum and lakefront, and the Wisconsin Dells (about 3 hours) is the waterpark classic. For a three-day long weekend, Door County is the best summer shoreline trip, while St. Louis and Louisville offer affordable, kid-packed city breaks. The right answer mostly depends on how many days you have and the season.

How far can you realistically drive for a weekend from Chicago?

For a genuine two-day, Saturday-to-Sunday weekend, keep the one-way drive under about three and a half hours, so you still get a full day on each end rather than losing them to the road. That covers Milwaukee, Lake Geneva, Starved Rock, Galena and the Wisconsin Dells. Places four to five hours out, like Door County, St. Louis and Louisville, are better as three-day long weekends with a Friday or Monday added.

What are the closest weekend getaways to Chicago?

The closest family-friendly escapes are all about 90 minutes out, Milwaukee and Lake Geneva to the north and Starved Rock State Park to the southwest. Milwaukee is the easy city pick, Lake Geneva is the lake-resort town, and Starved Rock is the outdoor option with canyon and waterfall trails. Galena, Illinois, a bit farther at under three hours, is another classic short hop.

What is a good weekend trip from Chicago in winter?

Winter favors indoor and year-round destinations. The Wisconsin Dells is the top pick, since its big resort waterparks are kept warm all year, and Milwaukee's museums and zoo work in any weather. Lake Geneva has an indoor-waterpark resort too. For a festive December long weekend, Frankenmuth, Michigan, a Christmas town about four and a half hours east, is worth the drive.

Where should families go for a three-day weekend from Chicago?

With three days the four-to-five-hour destinations open up. Door County, Wisconsin is the best choice for summer, with calm bay beaches and harbor towns. St. Louis is a strong value, thanks to its free zoo, science center and art museum, plus the Gateway Arch and the City Museum. Louisville, Kentucky is the quirky, affordable pick, with the Mega Cavern, the Slugger Museum and more.

Is the Wisconsin Dells a good weekend trip from Chicago?

Yes, it is one of the best. The Dells is about a three-hour drive straight up I-90, and its waterpark resorts turn the hotel into the whole vacation, which is ideal for younger kids. Because the indoor parks are warm year-round, it works as a two-day or three-day trip in any season. Book the resort early for summer and holiday weekends, when rooms sell out and rates climb.
