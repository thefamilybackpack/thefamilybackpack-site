Door County, Wisconsin · Cherry Picking

Door County cherry picking, with kids

Door County is the tart-cherry capital of the Midwest, and picking your own is one of the great cheap family mornings of a Wisconsin summer. The catch is timing: the season is short, it swings weeks with the weather, and the orchards are not all equal for little kids. Here is how to get it right.

The short answerTL;DR

Door County's signature crop is the tart Montmorency cherry, and u-pick runs roughly early July through mid-August: sweet cherries come first (early July, short windows that can sell out in days), then the tarts (mid-July into early August). For families, the most kid-friendly orchards are Hyline (a shuttle to the trees and a stroller-friendly path, in Fish Creek), Seaquist (a big shaded playground near Sister Bay) and Choice (low trees kids can actually reach, in Sturgeon Bay). The one rule everyone learns the hard way: call the orchard or check its Facebook the morning you go, because dates move and pick-your-own opens and closes fast.

Door County grows more tart cherries than almost anywhere in the country, and for a few weeks every summer you can drive onto a family orchard, grab a pail, and pick them yourself for a few dollars a quart. It is one of the best-value family activities on the peninsula: hours outdoors, a bucket of fruit to take home, and the kind of simple, hands-on afternoon kids actually remember. But cherry picking rewards planning. The season is narrow and shifts with the spring weather, sweet and tart cherries ripen at different times, and the orchard that is magic with a toddler is not always the one your map sends you to first.

The timing · Get this right firstWhen is cherry season in Door County?

There are really two cherry seasons here, back to back. Sweet cherries (the Bing and Rainier types you eat fresh) come first, usually ripening around early July and running a week or two. Their u-pick windows are short, and popular orchards can sell out of pick-your-own sweets in a matter of days. Then the star of Door County, the tart Montmorency, follows about two weeks later, with pick-your-own typically open from mid July into early or mid August. Any single orchard's tart picking often lasts only one to three weeks, so the window you actually have at a given farm is narrower than the season sounds.

Geography matters more than people expect on a peninsula this long. Orchards around Sturgeon Bay at the south ripen first; the orchards up north around Sister Bay and Ellison Bay run a few days to a week later, and can still have tarts into the first or second week of August. If you miss the window down south, driving north can buy you a few more days.

Field note

Why you cannot just book a date: the Door County cherry season is set by the spring. A late frost can kill buds (it hurt the 2026 crop), a cold or wet spring keeps the bees from pollinating, a cloudy June delays ripening, and a hot, sunny stretch speeds everything up by days. That is why one year opens July 4 and the next barely gets going by July 12. Two reliable ways to time it: watch Destination Door County's cherry season tracker (the Cherry Blossom and harvest report), and follow your target orchards on Facebook, where they post real-time "picking today" updates. Treat any date in this guide as typical, not promised.

The orchards · Where to actually goThe best u-pick orchards for families

Door County has dozens of orchards, but only some offer true pick-your-own, and fewer still are set up for little kids. Below are the family picks, the ones that combine real u-pick with playgrounds, shade, low trees or a ride out to the rows. A quick word on prices: the big orchards (Seaquist, Choice, Lautenbach's) do not publish per-pail prices online, so phone is your only reliable source. Everyone sells pre-picked and often pre-pitted cherries too, if the kids lose interest after ten minutes.

The top family pickHyline Orchard, Fish Creek

If you are picking with young kids, start with Hyline Orchard on Highway 42 near Fish Creek. It is the most toddler-friendly setup on the peninsula: staff drive you out to the rows on a six-seat cart rather than making little legs walk, the picking path is paved and stroller-friendly, and back at the market there is a swing set and a bounce house with a slide for when attention spans run out. They pick tart Montmorency and Bing sweet cherries across 150 acres, and recently charged around $16 for a four-quart pail (confirm the current price, it has been climbing). Pre-picked and pre-pitted cherries, homemade pies and pressed cherry juice are all in the market too. Phone ahead: 920-868-3067.

The big one · Best amenitiesSeaquist Orchards, near Sister Bay

Up north between Sister Bay and Ellison Bay, Seaquist Orchards is the giant of Door County cherries, roughly a thousand acres of tart trees, and it is built to entertain a family. The big draw for little kids is the large shaded playground with a sandbox and a pedal-car track, so one parent can pick while the other holds down the play area. There is a bakery, viewing windows into the canning kitchen, and shelves of jams and pie fillings. U-pick runs on limited days in season, so this is one to call about (920-854-4199). Time it for late July and you may catch the Seaquist cherry festival, with a bounce house, face painting, wagon tours and pit-spitting.

Best for little armsChoice Orchards, Sturgeon Bay

At the south end near Sturgeon Bay, Choice Orchards has the clever detail that matters most with small children: the trees are kept low, so a four-year-old can actually reach the cherries instead of being hoisted up. They pick both sweet and tart (sweets from around July 7, tarts from around July 15, each lasting about three weeks), and the farm doubles as an outing with a 10-acre evergreen maze, a playground, picnic tables and a small orchard museum in a restored picker's shack. On-site pitting is available for a fee. Open daily in season, roughly 9 to 5; call 920-743-8980.

Two more worth the driveLautenbach's and Meleddy

Lautenbach's Orchard Country in Fish Creek is the easy all-in-one: pick-your-own tart cherries on a big farm, plus a playground, a permanent pit-spitting lane, a bakery and a winery with tastings for the adults. Its "Summer Harvest Cherry Fest" in July adds wagon rides and pie-eating. Call 920-868-3479. For something smaller and more old-fashioned, Meleddy Cherry Farm near Forestville in the south is a simple, scenic tart-cherry farm whose main attraction is watching your cherries run through a historic pitting machine right in front of you. Recent pricing was about $20 a pail to pick, plus $4 to $5 to pit, and it is cash or check only, with a season that often lasts just a week or two. A few other smaller u-pick farms (Robertson and Alexander's among them) open each year; all of them live and die by Facebook updates.

From our family · picking with a toddler

The thing we learned picking tart cherries with a little one: pick the orchard for the setup, not the fruit, because the cherries are good everywhere. What actually decides the morning is whether there is a ride out to the trees instead of a long walk, whether the branches are low enough for small hands, and whether there is a playground or a bounce house to pivot to when the picking ends after fifteen enthusiastic minutes. A four-quart pail is plenty for one family, and honestly most of it gets filled by the grown-ups while the kids eat a few and wander off, which is exactly how it should go.

The practical bitWhat to bring, and how to get cherries home

Cherry picking is low-gear, but a few things make it smoother with kids. Bring sun hats, sunscreen and water, since the rows are open and July in Wisconsin gets hot. Wear clothes that can take cherry-juice stains. Most orchards hand you a pail; some (like Robertson) give you a pail to keep. And here is the one that catches people out: tart cherries are highly perishable, so bring a cooler for the drive home, especially if you are not heading straight back. In the fridge they last only a day or two.

To keep them, pit before you freeze, never after: freezing with the pits in creates ice crystals that turn the fruit mushy and make pitting a nightmare once thawed. Many orchards will run your haul through a pitting machine on-site for a few dollars a pail, which is well worth it with a big pick. At home, bag them flat in freezer bags (around three cups per quart bag), pour in any juice to guard against freezer burn, and freeze the bags lying flat before stacking. Frozen this way, Door County tarts keep for about a year, ready for pies and crisps all winter.

Know before you pickSweet vs tart, and what to do with each

The two cherries are for two different jobs. Sweet cherries (Bing, Rainier) are the ones you eat straight from the pail; their season is short and they do not keep or bake especially well. The tart Montmorency, Door County's signature, is too sour for most kids to eat raw, but it is the queen of the kitchen: pies, cobblers, crisps, jam, preserves, juice and drying. This is why nearly all of Door County's u-pick and its whole cherry industry centers on tarts. If your plan is to bake and freeze, aim for the tart window in mid-to-late July; if you just want a sweet snack and a photo, the early-July sweets are the move.

Make a weekend of itCherry festivals and a full family day

Cherry picking pairs naturally with the rest of a Door County trip. The big community event is the Jacksonport Cherry Fest at Lakeside Park, held the first Saturday of August (Saturday, August 1 in 2026), free to attend, with cherry kolaches and pie, a car show, crafts, live music and kids' activities. Individual orchards run their own festivals in late July (Seaquist and Lautenbach's above). One point of confusion worth clearing up: the famous National Cherry Festival is in Traverse City, Michigan, not Door County, so do not go looking for it here.

Build the picking into a classic peninsula day: a cherry orchard in the cool of the morning, a calm bay beach in the afternoon (see our best Door County family beaches), and cherry-everything for dessert, pie, custard and cherry-stuffed sweets, from the county's family restaurants and treat stops. Cherry picking day checklist
  • Call the orchard or check its Facebook the morning you go (dates and "open today" status move fast)
  • Aim for mid-to-late July for tart Montmorency; early July if you want sweets
  • Bring sun hats, sunscreen, water and stain-friendly clothes
  • Pack a cooler for the drive home; tarts spoil fast
  • Pay the few dollars for on-site pitting if you pick a big haul
  • Pit before freezing, then freeze the bags flat
  • Pick the orchard for its kid setup (cart ride, low trees, playground), not just the fruit

Before you go

When is cherry season in Door County?

Roughly early July through mid-August. Sweet cherries ripen first (early to mid July, in short windows that can sell out in days), then the tart Montmorency cherries, the main pick-your-own crop, run from about mid-July into early or mid August. Southern orchards near Sturgeon Bay ripen a few days before the northern ones near Sister Bay. Dates swing with the spring weather, so confirm with the orchard before you go.

When are the tart Montmorency cherries ready to pick?

Usually from the middle of July into early August, following the sweet cherries by about two weeks. Any single orchard's tart picking often lasts only one to three weeks, and northern orchards can run into the first or second week of August. Watch Destination Door County's harvest report and the orchards' Facebook pages for the current year's dates.

Which Door County orchards let you pick your own cherries?

Confirmed pick-your-own orchards include Hyline (Fish Creek), Seaquist (near Sister Bay, limited days), Choice (Sturgeon Bay), Lautenbach's Orchard Country (Fish Creek), Meleddy (Forestville), Robertson (Sturgeon Bay) and Alexander's (Brussels). Some places, like Country Ovens (Cherry De-Lite) and Wood Orchard, are buy-only markets rather than u-pick, so check before you drive.

How much does cherry picking cost in Door County?

Expect roughly $16 to $20 for a pail (a pail is 4 to 5 quarts, very roughly 6 to 8 pounds), with on-site pitting adding a few dollars. Hyline recently charged about $16 a four-quart pail and Meleddy about $20 plus pitting. Prices rise every year and the big orchards do not post them online, so call ahead for the current rate.

What are the best Door County orchards for toddlers and little kids?

Hyline in Fish Creek is the most toddler-friendly, with a cart ride out to the trees, a stroller-friendly paved path and a bounce house. Choice in Sturgeon Bay keeps its trees low so small children can reach the cherries, and Seaquist near Sister Bay has a big shaded playground so one parent can pick while the other supervises.

What is the difference between sweet and tart cherries?

Sweet cherries (Bing, Rainier) are for eating fresh and have a short season in early July. Tart Montmorency cherries, Door County's signature, are too sour to eat raw for most people but are the best cherries for pies, jam, juice and freezing. Nearly all of Door County's u-pick is tart cherries.

How do you freeze Door County cherries?

Pit them first, never freeze with the pits in, then bag them flat in freezer bags (about three cups per quart bag) with any juice poured in to prevent freezer burn, and freeze the bags lying flat before stacking. Many orchards will machine-pit your haul for a few dollars. Frozen this way, tart cherries keep for about a year.

Is there a Door County cherry festival?

Yes. The Jacksonport Cherry Fest at Lakeside Park is held the first Saturday of August (August 1 in 2026), free to attend, with cherry treats, a car show, crafts and kids' activities. Several orchards, including Seaquist and Lautenbach's, also run their own smaller cherry festivals in late July. Note that the National Cherry Festival is in Traverse City, Michigan, not Door County.
