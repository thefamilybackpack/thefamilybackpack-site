Midwest family vacations, by what fits your family

Most lists of Midwest family vacations are a random pile of cities with a paragraph each. That is not how you choose. The Midwest is a road-trip region, not one destination, so the real question is what kind of trip fits your family right now: little kids, a city weekend, a tight budget, a waterpark, the outdoors or a rainy-day escape. Pick the situation, and the destination picks itself.

The short answerTL;DR

There is no single best Midwest family vacation, there is the one that fits your family. For little kids, go to Door County for calm, shallow water. For an easy city weekend, Milwaukee. On a budget, St. Louis, where the zoo, science center and art museum are all free. For a waterpark, the Wisconsin Dells. For something they cannot do anywhere else, Louisville and its underground zipline cavern. Pick the situation below, and go deep rather than wide.

The mistake families make with the Midwest is looking for one headline destination, the way you would with Orlando or the Grand Canyon. That is not how this region rewards you. Its gift is density: a peninsula of state parks, a city with a top-five zoo, the waterpark capital of the country and a city where the zoo, the science center and the art museum are all free, and all within a day's drive of each other. So the useful way to choose is not a ranked list of cities. It is to start with your family's situation this year, and let that point you at the right trip. Here are the best Midwest family vacations, sorted the way you actually decide.

Mapped · Midwest family trips, end to end

With little kids · The gentle oneBest for toddlers and babies

With a one-year-old or a toddler, you want calm water, short drives and a slow pace, which points at Door County, the long Wisconsin peninsula with shallow, calm bay beaches, a state-park tower with a ramp instead of only stairs, and harbor towns you can base in and barely drive. For a rainy or cold stretch with little kids, pair it with a Great Wolf Lodge or another indoor waterpark, which run a warm toddler zone year-round (more in our best Midwest waterparks guide). The rule with babies is one base, short loops, and water you are not nervous about.

The easy city break · CompactBest for a city weekend

A walkable Great Lakes lakefront

When you want a city that does not overwhelm a family, go to Milwaukee. It is compact, sits right on Lake Michigan, and is anchored by one of the country's best zoos (which runs several Family Free Days a year), plus hands-on museums and a free lakefront beach, all without a theme-park budget. It is the easiest of the Midwest cities to reach and to get around, which matters more than it sounds when you are herding small children between stops. See where to stay in Milwaukee with kids for the best family bases.

On a budget · The value championBest for a cheap family vacation

The Midwest's best-kept budget secret is St. Louis, where the headline attractions are genuinely free: the Saint Louis Zoo, the Science Center, the Art Museum and the History Museum all cost nothing to enter. Add the one paid must-do, the wild, part-playground City Museum, and you can fill a long weekend and spend almost nothing on admissions. For a family watching every dollar, nowhere else in the region comes close, and it works year-round because the headliners are indoors. For the best-value bases, see where to stay in St. Louis with kids.

For the waterpark trip · Year-roundBest for a waterpark vacation

If the kids just want to get wet, the Wisconsin Dells is the undisputed answer, marketed as the Waterpark Capital of the World, with the largest indoor and the largest outdoor waterparks in America within a few miles of each other. The indoor resorts run warm year-round, so this is a February trip as much as a July one. The whole decision there is which resort fits your family, which we break down in our Dells waterpark resort comparison and the wider best Midwest waterparks guide.

Something they cannot do anywhere else · Big kidsBest for an unforgettable one-off

When your kids are old enough and you want the trip they will still talk about years later, go to Louisville for the Mega Cavern, a former limestone mine under the city that holds the world's only fully underground zipline course, plus an underground ropes challenge and a tram tour that works for younger ones too. Pair it with the giant Louisville Slugger bat and the hands-on science center and you have a weekend built on things they genuinely cannot do anywhere else. Just south of the Midwest proper, but an easy add from the region.

For a rainy or winter escapeBest when the weather will not cooperate

The Midwest's weather is half the planning, so it helps to know the trips that work when it is cold or pouring. Indoor waterpark resorts (the Dells, or a Great Wolf near a city) turn a snowy weekend into a warm one. The big indoor zoos, led by Omaha's Henry Doorly with its rainforest dome, are great in any weather, and in December many Midwest zoos become some of the best holiday-light events in the country (see our best Midwest zoos guide). And the free, indoor city headliners, the City Museum and the St. Louis museums, do not care what the sky is doing.

The famous names · Worth knowingThe bigger destinations beyond our core

The Midwest has more marquee family destinations than any one site can cover in depth, and a few belong on your radar even though we do not yet have a full guide for each. Mackinac Island, Michigan is a car-free island of bikes, fudge and a grand old hotel. The Black Hills of South Dakota pair Mount Rushmore with Custer State Park's bison. Cedar Point in Sandusky, Ohio is a roller-coaster capital with its own waterparks, and nearby Hocking Hills adds Ohio waterfalls and cabins, a reminder that Ohio is a genuine family-travel state the big lists often skip. Branson, Missouri is shows and Silver Dollar City; Minneapolis has the Mall of America; and Traverse City, Michigan is the Great Lakes beach-and-cherry trip. Treat these as the next tier once you have done the core.

How to plan a Midwest family vacation
  • Pick the situation first (toddlers, city, budget, waterpark, outdoors, rainy), then the destination, not the other way around.
  • Base in one place and do short loops. The region is drivable, but distances are deceptive, so resist stringing five cities together.
  • Match the season. Outdoor trips (Door County, outdoor waterparks) are June to August; cities and indoor waterparks work year-round.
  • Lean on the free cities (St. Louis) and zoo Family Free Days to cut the budget hard.
  • Book summer and holiday weeks early, especially Dells resorts and anything with a waterpark.
  • Have a rainy-day backup for every trip: an indoor waterpark, a zoo animal house, or a free museum.
From our family · how we actually travel the Midwest

We do not try to see the Midwest in one big loop. We treat it as a list of long weekends and work through it a trip at a time, picking the next one by whatever stage the kids are at. With a baby it was Door County; the city weekends and the waterparks came as they got older. That is the whole trick: one base, one long weekend, matched to the kids you have right now.

Attractions, free-day schedules, prices, hours and seasonal openings all change, and some destinations here are covered in depth on their own guides while others are a starting point only. Confirm the current details on the linked guides and each venue's official site before you book.

Before you go

What is the best Midwest family vacation?

It depends on your family. For toddlers, Door County has calm, shallow water and short drives. For an easy city weekend, Milwaukee. On a budget, St. Louis, where the zoo, science center and art museum are all free. For a waterpark, the Wisconsin Dells. For an unforgettable one-off, Louisville and its underground zipline cavern. Pick by your kids' ages and the season rather than chasing a single best.

What is the best Midwest family vacation on a budget?

St. Louis is the Midwest's value champion because its headline attractions are free to enter: the Saint Louis Zoo, the Science Center, the Art Museum and the History Museum. Add the City Museum as the one paid must-do and you can fill a long weekend spending almost nothing on admissions. Elsewhere, lean on zoo Family Free Days and a reciprocal zoo membership to cut costs.

When is the best time for a Midwest family vacation?

June through August is peak season for the outdoor trips, Door County beaches, outdoor waterparks and the lakes, with warm weather and full schedules. The cities (Milwaukee, St. Louis, Louisville) and the indoor waterparks and zoos work year-round, since their headliners do not depend on the weather, and winter brings lower rates plus zoo holiday-light events.

What is the best Midwest family vacation for toddlers?

Door County is the easiest with toddlers and babies: shallow, calm bay water, an accessible tower with a ramp, and short drives if you base in one town. Pair it with an indoor waterpark resort like Great Wolf Lodge, which runs a warm toddler zone year-round, for a rainy-day or winter backup. Keep it to one base and short loops with little kids.

Can you do a Midwest family vacation as a road trip?

Yes, the Midwest is a road-trip region and its destinations are drivable from one another, but distances are deceptive, so the smarter move with kids is to base in one place and do short day loops rather than stringing several cities into one trip. Pick one destination per long weekend and go deep, and save the multi-stop road trip for older kids who travel well.
