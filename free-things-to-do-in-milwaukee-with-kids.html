Free things to do in Milwaukee, with kids

You can fill days in Milwaukee with kids without buying a ticket: a real Lake Michigan beach, free museum days, the art museum free for under-12s, and even six free zoo days a year. Here's how to do the city for next to nothing.

The short answerTL;DR

Milwaukee is a genuinely cheap city with kids if you plan a little. The lakefront and Bradford Beach are free all summer. The Art Museum is free for kids under 12 every day. Both Betty Brinn and the Public Museum have a standing free day each month, and the County Zoo has six Family Free Days a year. Stack a couple of these and you've filled a trip without a ticket.

A family trip to Milwaukee doesn't have to be expensive. Between a real city beach, a lakefront full of free parks, and a surprising number of free museum days, you can put together a couple of full days here and barely open your wallet. Here's what's free, and when.

Always freeThe beach and the lakefront

Bradford Beach on a summer afternoon

The best free thing in Milwaukee is the water. Bradford Beach is a genuine Lake Michigan swimming beach with summer lifeguards and enough sand for a full afternoon, and it costs nothing. From there, the lakefront path runs past a string of free parks and playgrounds, the art museum and Discovery World, all walkable or a short stroller push apart. On a warm day, this alone can be the whole trip, and it's the kind of open-ended outdoor time kids love most.

Free with timingThe museum free days

Several of Milwaukee's museums have a standing free day, so a little planning saves real money.

Field note

The Milwaukee Art Museum is free for kids 12 and under every day (and Wisconsin K–12 teachers). Betty Brinn is free the third Thursday of each month; the Milwaukee Public Museum is free the first Thursday (Kohl's Thank You Thursday). And the County Zoo has six Family Free Days in 2026: Jan 3, Feb 7, Mar 7, Oct 3, Nov 7, Dec 5 (parking is still $15). Free-day capacity fills, so arrive early.

From our family · travelling with a one-year-old

We build budget trips around the free days without letting them run the whole plan. If our dates line up with a free zoo Saturday or a free-Thursday museum, great, we bank the saving. But with a toddler we won't twist the whole trip around a date; the beach and the lakefront are free every day and honestly the easiest wins anyway. Free is a bonus, not the boss.

The reliable standbysPlaygrounds and parks

A lakefront community playground

When you just need to let kids run, Milwaukee's parks deliver for free. The Northwestern Mutual Community Park playground on the lakefront is a standout, and Lakeshore State Park's trails give you an easy, free walk with big water views right downtown. Add the free downtown Holiday Lights Festival (November through January) in winter, and there's a no-cost option in every season.

Before you go

What free things are there to do in Milwaukee with kids?

The lakefront and Bradford Beach (free all summer), the lakefront parks and playgrounds, and the Milwaukee Art Museum (free for kids 12 and under every day). Betty Brinn and the Public Museum each have a free day monthly, and the County Zoo has six Family Free Days a year (parking still $15).

When is the Milwaukee zoo free in 2026?

The Milwaukee County Zoo has six Family Free Days in 2026: January 3, February 7, March 7, October 3, November 7 and December 5, when admission is free for everyone. Parking is still $15. There's also Frosty Free Week (December 26 to 30) with free daytime admission.

Are Milwaukee museums ever free?

Yes. The Milwaukee Art Museum is free every day for children 12 and under. Betty Brinn is free the third Thursday of each month, and the Milwaukee Public Museum is free the first Thursday (Kohl's Thank You Thursday, permanent exhibits). Arrive early, since free-day capacity can fill.

Is Milwaukee a cheap city for a family trip?

It can be. Between a free Lake Michigan beach, free lakefront parks and playgrounds, kids-under-12-free at the art museum, and standing free days at the zoo and museums, you can fill a couple of days with almost no admission cost. Book a hotel with free breakfast and an indoor pool and you cut costs further.
