The picksWhat to actually buy

Best overall for flying: the No Reception Club Getaway Bag (around $235). It is the one built from the ground up for travel rather than for the nursery. At 24 litres and 3.4 pounds it is sized to pass as a personal item, the clamshell opening and side window mean you grab a diaper without unpacking, and the luggage sleeve, wet compartment and hidden passport pocket are exactly what you want at a gate. It is expensive, and it is the one most flying parents end up wishing they had bought first.

Best organized: the BÉIS Ultimate Diaper Backpack (around $198). A 24.4 litre bag with a fold-out changing station, a removable fanny pack for quick gate runs, insulated bottle pockets and a trolley sleeve. If you like a place for everything, this is the pick.

Best value: the Skip Hop Forma Next (around $73 to $79). The sweet spot of price and travel features: packing cubes, an insulated bottle cube, stroller straps and, on the Next version, a luggage pass-through sleeve the original Forma lacks. For most families this is enough bag.

Best budget: the Ruvalino Large (around $45). Cavernous, waterproof, sixteen pockets and an included changing pad. It is not elegant and the straps are basic, but it survives travel and costs a quarter of the premium picks, which is why it keeps winning budget roundups.

Best wipe-clean and style: the Dagne Dover Indi (around $185 to $250). Neoprene that sponges clean, a laptop sleeve, a luggage sleeve and a look that does not read as a diaper bag. Lower stated capacity than the boxy bags, so it suits lighter packers and shorter trips.

Best for dads: the Fifty Fifty Gear Steady 26L (around $199). A rugged, large, muted-colour travel pack designed for parents who do not want a floral tote. Roomy enough for two kids' gear, though its size can be snug under a smaller seat.

Best for two kids or twins: the Itzy Ritzy Boss Plus (around $200). Nineteen pockets, stands upright on its own and splits cleanly so one side is newborn and the other is toddler. Big, so pack it with the under-seat limit in mind.

Best compact or minimalist: the Itzy Ritzy Mini Plus (around $100). When you are past the newborn stage, a ten-pocket mini backpack with a changing pad is all you need, and it disappears under any seat. Many light packers end up here.

From our family · what we learned

The lesson from flying with a baby is that the bag matters less than how you load it. The best travel diaper bag in the world is useless if everything you need mid-flight is at the bottom. We pack a small pouch with two or three diapers, a travel pack of wipes, a changing mat and one spare outfit, keep that under the seat, and put the rest in the main bag overhead. A clamshell or side-access bag earns its price here, because you are opening it in a space the size of a dinner tray.

The part nobody explainsDoes a diaper bag count as a carry-on?

This is where families get caught at the gate, and where almost every "best diaper bag" list goes quiet. The short version: when you are traveling with a baby or toddler, many US airlines let you bring a diaper bag for free in addition to your carry-on and personal item, but not all of them, and the rules differ again outside the US. The under-seat rule

Whatever the allowance, the bag you keep with you needs to fit under the seat in front of you, because that is where it lives during takeoff, landing and any time the seatbelt sign is on. Most US personal items run about 18 by 14 by 8 inches, and most travel diaper backpacks in the 20 to 25 litre range fit with room to spare. If a carrier counts the diaper bag as your carry-on instead, the usual ceiling is about 22 by 14 by 9 inches. Budget and foreign airlines are stricter, so measure before a long-haul trip.

Beyond the bagWhat else you get when you fly with a baby

A lap infant comes with more than a baggage headache. Every airline here gives you some version of these, though the details differ, so it is worth knowing what to ask for. A stroller or pushchair is almost always free: you gate-check it at the aircraft door on US carriers and most others, a slim one-piece pushchair is often handed back at the door, and a larger one comes out at the carousel. A car seat is free to check nearly everywhere; to use it in the cabin you have to buy a seat for the child, and it must be an aircraft-approved seat with the right label, which is the safest way to fly a baby on a long trip.

A bassinet, or sky cot, is the one worth planning for. It is offered only on long-haul widebody flights, is never guaranteed, and has to be requested when you book rather than at the gate. Weight limits run roughly 9 to 11 kg (about 20 to 24 pounds): Delta calls it a SkyCot, Emirates and Lufthansa cap it around 11 kg, and the narrowbody US carriers (Southwest, Alaska, JetBlue, Spirit and Frontier) do not offer one at all. Most US airlines also let families with under-2s pre-board, and several foreign carriers board infants first, which is the easy moment to install a car seat before the aisle fills up.

On cost, a lap infant flies free on US domestic routes and usually runs about 10 percent of the adult fare plus taxes on international ones; European budget carriers charge a flat fee instead, around 25 pounds each way on Ryanair and easyJet. And on liquids, formula, breast milk, pumped milk and baby food are all allowed through security above the normal limit when you travel with an infant, along with ice packs to keep them cold. Declare them at the checkpoint and they are screened separately.

At the airportHow to pack and survive the flight

A few things make the difference between a calm boarding and a meltdown in the jet bridge. Use the two-bag method: a small quick-access pouch under the seat, the bulk supplies overhead. Pack more diapers than the trip needs, because delays are when you run out. Bring scented diaper sacks, since an airplane lavatory is a sealed box and changing tables are tiny. Carry a spare shirt for yourself, not just the baby. And at security, tell the officer you have formula, breast milk or baby food; these are allowed above the normal liquid limit when you travel with an infant, along with ice packs to keep them cold, but they are screened separately.

Before you fly

Does a diaper bag count as a carry-on or a personal item?

It depends on the airline. When you are traveling with a baby or toddler, American and United generally let you bring one diaper bag per child for free, in addition to your own carry-on and personal item. Delta and Southwest usually count the diaper bag as your personal item or part of your carry-on allowance. Policies change and vary by fare, so confirm with your airline before you fly.

What size diaper bag can you take on a plane?

Aim for a bag that fits under the seat in front of you. Most US airline personal items are roughly 18 by 14 by 8 inches, and most travel diaper backpacks in the 20 to 25 litre range slide under the seat easily. If a carrier counts the diaper bag as a carry-on, the usual limit is about 22 by 14 by 9 inches. Exact sizes vary by airline and aircraft.

What is the best diaper bag for flying?

For most flying families the best all-round pick is a 20 to 25 litre travel diaper backpack with a luggage pass-through sleeve, quick side access and a wipe-clean lining. The No Reception Club Getaway Bag and the BÉIS Ultimate Diaper Backpack are the strongest travel-built options, the Skip Hop Forma Next is the best value, and the Ruvalino Large is the best budget choice.

Can you bring a diaper bag through airport security?

Yes. A diaper bag goes through the X-ray like any carry-on. Formula, breast milk, water for mixing and baby food are allowed in reasonable quantities above the usual 3.4 ounce liquid limit when you are traveling with an infant or toddler; tell the TSA officer so they can screen them separately. Ice packs to keep milk cool are allowed too.

How do you pack a diaper bag for a flight?

Use a two-bag approach. Keep a small pouch with two or three diapers, a travel pack of wipes, a changing mat, a spare outfit and a snack within reach under the seat, and stash the bulk supplies in the main bag in the overhead bin. Pack a change of clothes for you, scented diaper sacks for the cramped lavatory, and more diapers than you think you need for delays.

What is the best diaper bag for international travel?

For long-haul and international trips, favour a bag that genuinely fits a strict personal item, since budget and some foreign carriers are less generous than US airlines. A compact 20 to 24 litre backpack with a luggage sleeve, a document sleeve and a hidden passport pocket, like the No Reception Club Getaway Bag or a Dagne Dover Indi, travels best. Always check the infant baggage rules for your specific airline, as they differ widely outside the US.
