Summer getaways from Chicago, with kids

Summer is when the trips near Chicago come alive: sugar-sand Lake Michigan beaches, dunes you can climb, waterparks indoor and out, and the shoreline towns of Door County. Here are the best family summer getaways from the city, from a one-hour beach day to a week up north, sorted so you can match one to your crew.

Summer is the payoff season for Chicago families. The lake that is brutal in January turns into a playground, and within an easy drive you can be on a wide sand beach, climbing a dune, floating a lazy river or exploring a harbor town. The only hard part is choosing, because unlike winter, almost everything is open and good. Here are the best family summer getaways from Chicago, grouped by the kind of trip you want, from a quick beach day to a full week up north, with honest drive times so the driving never swallows the fun.

For a beach dayThe Indiana Dunes, an hour out

The closest great beach day is the Indiana Dunes, about an hour east around the lake, where wide swimming beaches sit below sand dunes kids can climb. Do the dune scramble in the cool morning, then spend the afternoon on the sand near the lifeguards. It is cheap, it is close, and it packs a beach and an adventure into one day, see our Indiana Dunes with kids guide. For an easy day trip, it is the summer default.

For a weekendLake Geneva and the Michigan beach towns

For an overnight, two directions stand out. North, Lake Geneva, Wisconsin (90 minutes) is the classic lake-resort weekend, with Riviera Beach, boat tours, a drive-through safari and resort pools, see our Lake Geneva with kids guide. East, across the lake in Michigan, Saugatuck and Holland (about 2.5 hours) are the best beach-town weekend in range, with the top-ranked Oval Beach, the famous dune-buggy rides, a hand-cranked ferry and, in Holland, a Dutch windmill, see our Saugatuck and Holland with kids guide. Both give you proper Lake Michigan beach time with a town to come back to each evening.

For waterparksThe Wisconsin Dells in summer

Summer is when the Wisconsin Dells (about three hours) is at full strength, because the mega-resorts open their huge outdoor waterparks on top of the year-round indoor ones, and the famous Duck boat tours and go-karts run. It is the ultimate high-energy waterpark week for families who want slides over sand. Closer to home, the indoor and outdoor waterparks near Chicago (Great Wolf Gurnee and others) are an easy weekend too. See our guides to the Dells with kids and water parks near Chicago.

The big oneDoor County, worth a week

If you have more than a weekend, the standout summer trip is Door County, the peninsula north of Green Bay, about four to five hours up the shore. You get calm bay beaches, five state parks, cherry orchards, lighthouses and walkable harbor towns, the Midwest's best shoreline and a genuine change of pace. It earns a long weekend or a full week. Plan it with our Door County with kids guide, the best beaches and the Chicago to Door County drive guide.

Rounding it outA city trip and an outdoor day

Two more fill out a summer. Milwaukee (90 minutes) makes an easy city weekend, zoo, lakefront and museums, and one of the few trips where the train beats driving, see our Milwaukee with kids and Chicago to Milwaukee guides. And Starved Rock (90 minutes) is the best outdoor day, canyon and waterfall trails for active families, see our Starved Rock with kids guide. Between beaches, waterparks, a city and a hike, a Chicago summer has no shortage of easy family escapes.

The verdictHow to choose your summer

Match the trip to your time and your kids. Have a day? The Indiana Dunes for a beach, or Starved Rock for a hike. Have a weekend? Lake Geneva or the Michigan beach towns for sand, the Dells for waterparks, or Milwaukee for a city break. Have a week? Door County, the one that rewards real time. Book beach-town and Door County lodging early (summer weekends sell out), swim near the lifeguards on Lake Michigan, and you have a summer of easy, memorable family escapes without ever boarding a plane.

Summer getaway checklist
  • Match the trip to your time (a day, a weekend, or a week)
  • For a quick beach day, the Indiana Dunes are closest
  • For a week, Door County rewards the extra time
  • Swim near the lifeguards; Lake Michigan can have strong currents
  • Book beach-town and Door County lodging early for summer weekends
  • Leave early on summer Fridays to beat the traffic

Before you go

What are the best summer getaways from Chicago with kids?

For the closest beach-and-dunes day, the Indiana Dunes (about an hour). For a lake-resort weekend, Lake Geneva (90 minutes); for a beach-town weekend, Saugatuck and Holland in Michigan (2.5 hours). For waterparks, the Wisconsin Dells adds huge outdoor parks in summer. And the big one, worth a long weekend or a week, is Door County. Milwaukee and Starved Rock round out easy city and outdoor options.

Where are the best beaches near Chicago for a family trip?

The closest great beach-and-dunes day is the Indiana Dunes, about an hour east. For a beach-town weekend, Saugatuck and Holland in southwest Michigan have Oval Beach and Holland State Park (about 2.5 hours). Lake Geneva has a sandy town beach 90 minutes north, and Door County's calm bay beaches are the best for a longer trip. On Lake Michigan, always swim near the lifeguards.

What is the best summer trip from Chicago for young kids?

For toddlers and young kids, keep it close and simple. Try the Indiana Dunes for a beach day, Lake Geneva for a lake-resort weekend with a safari and boat tours, or an indoor-and-outdoor waterpark. These have gentle water, short drives and plenty of shade and backup. Save the longer Door County trip for when the kids can handle more car time.

How far should you drive for a summer getaway from Chicago with kids?

It depends on your time. For a day trip, stay within about 90 minutes (Indiana Dunes, Starved Rock, Lake Geneva). For a weekend, two to three hours opens up the Michigan beach towns and the Wisconsin Dells. For a week, Door County at four to five hours is worth the drive. Match the distance to how many days you have so the driving does not dominate.
