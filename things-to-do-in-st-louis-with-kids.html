St. Louis, Missouri · Field Guide No. 12

St. Louis with kids, wild and mostly free

St. Louis hands families two rare things: the City Museum, a climb-through art playground unlike anywhere else, and a stack of genuinely world-class attractions that cost nothing, the free zoo, science center and art museum all in one giant park.

The short answerTL;DR

Give St. Louis two or three days with kids. The one unmissable, and only real ticket you must buy, is the City Museum (about $20, plan four hours). Everything else can be nearly free: the Saint Louis Zoo, Science Center and Art Museum in Forest Park all have free admission, and the Gateway Arch grounds and museum are free too (only the tram to the top costs extra). Spring and fall are most comfortable.

Most cities give families one headline attraction. St. Louis gives you two kinds. First, the City Museum, which is genuinely unlike anywhere else on earth. Second, a rare wealth of free, world-class attractions, a free zoo, a free science center, a free art museum, all clustered in one enormous park. Plan the City Museum as your one big paid day, and fill the rest for almost nothing.

The headliner · Give it half a dayThe City Museum

Inside the City Museum’s tunnels and slides

Calling the City Museum a “museum” undersells it wildly. Housed in a 600,000-square-foot former shoe warehouse and built from salvaged architecture by artist Bob Cassilly, it's a climb-through, crawl-through, slide-through work of art: caves and tunnels, a ten-story slide, a three-story treehouse, a whale you climb inside, and the outdoor MonstroCity of repurposed planes and towers. Admission is about $20 a person (children under 3 free), the rooftop (with a Ferris wheel) is an extra weather-dependent fee, and parking runs $15 across the street. Plan four hours, minimum.

Field note

Know before you go: it's an active place, so wear closed-toe shoes and long pants (the tunnels scrape). Some slides, including the ten-story one, have a 48-inch height requirement. Buy tickets ahead, write your phone number on your kid's wristband (there's a spot for it at the front desk), and pick a meeting spot, it's easy to get separated in the maze.

From our family · travelling with a one-year-old

With a one-year-old, the City Museum is a mixed bag: much of it needs a climbing, crawling kid, not a baby. The saver is Toddler Town, a gentler area built for ages 6 and under, so one parent can take the baby there while the other chases an older sibling up the slides. Honestly, though, with a toddler our easiest St. Louis day is the free zoo, more on that next. The City Museum is a “come back when they're four” kind of place.

St. Louis’s superpower · FreeForest Park and the free attractions

The free Saint Louis Zoo

Here's what makes St. Louis special for families on a budget: Forest Park, bigger than New York's Central Park, holds several free, world-class attractions. The Saint Louis Zoo is one of the only major zoos in the country with free admission (parking about $20, or free farther out), with a famous sea-lion show and a historic flight cage. The Science Center (free) has hands-on exhibits and a planetarium; the Art Museum (free) sits atop Art Hill; and the Missouri History Museum (free) has an award-winning kids' History Clubhouse. You could fill two days here without paying admission once.

The icon · Book aheadThe Gateway Arch

The Gateway Arch and the Mississippi

The Gateway Arch, 630 feet of shining steel over the Mississippi, is a national park, and the grounds and museum below it are free. The museum tells the story of westward expansion with interactive exhibits kids can dig into. The one thing that costs extra is the tram to the top, a ride up inside the arch in a small enclosed pod to a panoramic view. It sells out daily, so book tram tickets well ahead. With little kids, the free museum and riverfront alone can be enough.

Getting around and where to base

St. Louis is a driving city, though the MetroLink light rail links the airport, downtown and Forest Park, handy for reaching the zoo, science center and art museum without parking. Most families base downtown near the Arch (close to the City Museum too) or in the Central West End (near Forest Park). Build a day around the City Museum, a day around Forest Park's free attractions, and the Arch into a downtown morning, and you've got a full, mostly-free St. Louis trip.

Before you go

What is the top thing to do in St. Louis with kids?

The City Museum, a one-of-a-kind climb-through playground and art installation built from salvaged architecture, with caves, tunnels, a ten-story slide and an outdoor MonstroCity. It's about $20 a person and worth four hours. After that, St. Louis is full of free family attractions like the zoo and Gateway Arch.

Is the Saint Louis Zoo in St. Louis really free?

Yes. The Saint Louis Zoo is one of the only major zoos in the country with free admission, year-round. You pay only for parking (about $20 a car, or park farther out for free) and optional extras like the train or special exhibits. It's a full free day, with a famous sea-lion show and a historic flight cage.

How much time do you need at the City Museum in St. Louis?

At least four hours, and many families spend five or six. It's huge (600,000 square feet across multiple levels plus a rooftop) and genuinely active, so wear closed-toe shoes and long pants. Some slides have a 48-inch height requirement, and there's a gentler Toddler Town for ages 6 and under.

What free things can families do in St. Louis?

A lot. In Forest Park (bigger than Central Park) the Saint Louis Zoo, Science Center, Art Museum and Missouri History Museum all have free admission. The Gateway Arch grounds and museum are free too (only the tram to the top costs extra). You can fill two days in St. Louis without paying admission once.

Do you need tickets for the Gateway Arch in St. Louis?

For the tram to the top, yes, and it sells out daily, so book ahead. The Arch grounds, the riverfront and the museum beneath the Arch (about westward expansion) are all free to visit. With young kids, the free museum and park can be plenty without the tram.
