Indianapolis, Indiana · With Kids

Things to do in Indianapolis with kids

Indianapolis is one of the most underrated family cities in the Midwest, home to the largest children's museum on the planet and a walkable downtown park packed with a zoo and museums. This is a visiting family's guide: what to do by your kids' ages, what it really costs, a two-day plan, and where to stay, from someone planning the trip, not just living there.

Indianapolis surprises families. It is flat, friendly and easy to drive, it has a compact downtown built around a canal and a green state park, and it happens to hold the single best thing a kid-focused city can have: the largest children's museum on earth. Add a strong zoo, a hands-on history park, a cluster of walkable downtown museums and a freshly reopened racing museum, and you have two or three days of genuinely good family time at Midwestern prices. This guide is written for families coming to visit, not residents, so it leads with what to do at your kids' ages, what each thing costs, how to string it into a plan, and where to stay to make it easy.

Getting thereWhere Indianapolis is, and the easy drive in

Indianapolis sits almost dead center in Indiana, at the crossing of several interstates, which is why it is such an easy family weekend. It is about a three-hour drive from Chicago down I-65, roughly two hours from both Cincinnati and Louisville, and about four hours from St. Louis. That central position makes it one of the better picks on our list of weekend getaways from Chicago, and the drive down I-65 is an easy one, mapped in our Chicago to Indianapolis guide. Once you arrive it is refreshingly simple: downtown is flat and compact, parking is cheap by big-city standards, and the attractions cluster in two spots, the downtown White River State Park area and a short drive north to the Children's Museum and Newfields.

The anchorThe Children's Museum of Indianapolis

This is the reason to come. The Children's Museum of Indianapolis is the largest children's museum in the world, five floors and about 7.5 acres of hands-on exhibits, and it genuinely earns the superlative. The headline draw is Dinosphere, one of the biggest displays of real dinosaur fossils anywhere, where kids can watch paleontologists work. Beyond it there is a beloved antique carousel you can ride, a full space and science wing, a Lego-style building zone, a dedicated Playscape area for toddlers and preschoolers, and the outdoor Sports Legends Experience, a whole sports campus with mini versions of real fields and a pedal-car track. Plan on most of a day; many families find one visit is not enough. Admission is dynamic (often around $29, sometimes less booked early online), and it is at 3000 N Meridian Street, a few minutes north of downtown.

A couple of tips that save the day with kids. Buy timed tickets online in advance, both to lock in the lower price and to guarantee entry on busy days. Arrive at opening, do the most popular exhibits (Dinosphere, the carousel) first while energy is high, and break for lunch before the afternoon meltdown. And do not try to see all five floors; let the kids lead and accept that you will miss things, which is a good reason to come back.

The key questionWhat to do by your kids' ages

The smartest way to plan Indianapolis is by age, because the city has something strong for every stage, and trying to do all of it with the wrong-aged kids is how trips go sideways.

Toddlers and preschoolers. Stay close to the hands-on and the outdoors. The Children's Museum's Playscape is built exactly for this age, the Indianapolis Zoo is a winner (especially the dolphin pool and the shark touch tank), and the downtown canal with its pedal boats is an easy, cheap afternoon. Keep days short and nap-friendly.

Elementary-age kids (roughly 5 to 11). This is Indy's sweet spot. The Children's Museum lands perfectly, Conner Prairie (hands-on 1800s history, animals and a tethered balloon ride) is a hit, the Indiana State Museum adds fossils and an IMAX, and Rhythm! Discovery Center, a hands-on drum-and-percussion museum downtown, is a cheap, underrated crowd-pleaser.

Tweens and teens. Older kids who have outgrown "kid" museums still like the Children's Museum's Sports Legends campus, and they get more out of the reopened Indianapolis Motor Speedway Museum, Newfields (art plus the outdoor 100 Acres park, good for a bike or a wander), and a Pacers or Indians game if the season fits.

Downtown, all walkableThe zoo, the canal and White River State Park

White River State Park is the thing that makes downtown Indianapolis so easy with kids: a green riverfront park, right in the city, that holds several attractions within a short walk. The Indianapolis Zoo is here, and it is unusual in combining a zoo, an aquarium-style Oceans exhibit with a shark touch tank, and a dolphin pool with in-water presentations, so it doubles as the city's answer to "is there an aquarium." Also in the park: the Indiana State Museum, the Eiteljorg Museum (Native American and Western art, with a strong family program), and the scenic Central Canal, where you can rent pedal boats or just walk the water's edge. You can easily spend a full, low-stress day inside this one park.

A short drive northConner Prairie and Newfields

Conner Prairie, in the northern suburb of Fishers (about 25 minutes from downtown), is one of the best family attractions in the state: a huge outdoor history park where costumed interpreters run an 1836 village, you can meet farm animals, climb a multi-story treehouse, and, in the warm months, ride a tethered helium balloon up over the grounds. It is hands-on in a way kids remember. Nearby, Newfields (home of the Indianapolis Museum of Art) is an art-and-nature campus whose family draw is the outdoors: The Garden and the 100 Acres art-and-nature park, with sculptures, trails and room to run. In winter it transforms for the walk-through Winterlights holiday display (see the winter section below).

For the racing fansThe reopened Motor Speedway Museum

The Indianapolis Motor Speedway Museum, on the grounds of the famous track, reopened in April 2025 after a major renovation, and it is far more interactive than the old one, with hands-on exhibits, racing simulators and rotating cars from the Indy 500's history. Track tours (availability varies by the racing calendar) can take you onto parts of the speedway and to kiss the famous Yard of Bricks. It is a quick, high-energy stop of an hour or two, best for kids six and up and anyone in the family who likes cars and speed.

Field note

The money-saver worth knowing: Indianapolis's big attractions (the Children's Museum and the zoo especially) use dynamic pricing, meaning the gate price rises on busy days and when you buy last minute. Booking online a few days ahead can save real money and locks in entry. If you plan to hit several paid attractions, check whether the Indy Attraction Pass works out cheaper for your mix before buying individual tickets. And watch for free days and library-pass programs; the Indianapolis library system, like many, lends out museum passes.

Rainy days and tight budgetsFree and indoor things to do

Indianapolis is kind to families watching the budget. The downtown canal and White River State Park are free to walk, the Indiana State Library and Soldiers and Sailors Monument (you can climb it for a small fee, or admire it for free) anchor Monument Circle, and Newfields offers free days to Indiana residents periodically and a free-to-roam outdoor park. For indoor backup on a cold or wet day, the Children's Museum, the Indiana State Museum (with its IMAX), the Eiteljorg and Rhythm! Discovery Center are all fully indoor. Several suburban public libraries and parks have free indoor play spaces, worth a search for your specific dates. It is easy to build a day here that costs almost nothing.

The cold monthsIndianapolis with kids in winter

Winter is a genuinely good time to visit, because the headline attractions are indoors and the city leans into the season. Newfields Winterlights turns the gardens into a large walk-through display of holiday lights (ticketed, popular, book ahead; confirm the current dates). The Children's Museum runs its Jolly Days Winter Wonderland with indoor "snow" play, and the whole downtown dresses up around Monument Circle, which becomes the "Circle of Lights." Pair an outdoor lights evening with indoor-museum days and winter becomes one of the easier times to do Indianapolis with kids, often with lower hotel rates too.

Where to sleepWhere to stay with kids

For a first family visit, base yourself downtown near White River State Park and the canal. From there the zoo, the park museums and the canal are walkable, the Children's Museum and Newfields are a short drive north, and you are close to restaurants. Downtown has a wide range of family-friendly hotels, and several have indoor pools, which is the single amenity that most reliably buys you a happy evening with kids after a long day of walking. If you would rather trade walkability for a pool-and-parking suburban base, the areas north toward Carmel and Fishers put you near Conner Prairie. Whatever you choose, book early for race weekends in May (the Indy 500) and big convention dates, when downtown rooms vanish and prices spike.

Putting it togetherA simple two-day Indianapolis plan

Day one, the Children's Museum. Go at opening and give it most of the day; it is the whole reason you came. In the late afternoon, drive back downtown, walk the canal and rent a pedal boat if the weather is good, and have an easy dinner. Day two, downtown and history. Spend the morning at the Indianapolis Zoo in White River State Park (dolphins and the shark touch tank first), then choose by age: the Indiana State Museum or Rhythm! Discovery Center for younger kids, or drive north to Conner Prairie for a big hands-on history afternoon. If you have a third day, add Newfields or the Motor Speedway Museum. Keep each day to one big thing plus one small thing, and you will not burn the kids out.

From our family · planning an Indy trip

What we would tell any family heading to Indianapolis: treat the Children's Museum as a full day, not a half, and do not schedule anything serious after it, because everyone will be happily wiped out. The second lesson is to lean on White River State Park. Having the zoo, two museums and the canal all in one walkable patch meant we could park once, go at the kids' pace, and bail to the hotel pool when we hit the wall, without a single stressful cross-town drive. Buy the museum and zoo tickets online the night before; we watched the gate price jump on a busy Saturday while ours was already locked in.

Indianapolis family trip checklist

Before you go

What are the best things to do in Indianapolis with kids?

The clear number one is The Children's Museum of Indianapolis, the largest children's museum in the world, which deserves most of a day. After that, the Indianapolis Zoo and the downtown canal in White River State Park, Conner Prairie for hands-on history, the Indiana State Museum, Newfields for art and nature, and the reopened Motor Speedway Museum. Plan around your kids' ages and you will not run out of things to do in two or three days.

What is the number one attraction in Indiana?

For families it is The Children's Museum of Indianapolis, which is the largest children's museum in the world and regularly ranked among the best in the country. It draws over a million visitors a year with exhibits like Dinosphere, a historic carousel and the outdoor Sports Legends sports campus. If you do only one thing in the state with kids, make it this.

Does Indianapolis have an aquarium?

There is no standalone public aquarium in Indianapolis, but the Indianapolis Zoo fills the gap. Its Oceans exhibit has a shark touch tank, and the zoo is well known for its dolphin pool with in-water presentations. For families hoping for an aquarium experience, the zoo is the place to go.

What are some hidden gems in Indianapolis for families?

A few that visitors miss. Rhythm! Discovery Center is a cheap, hands-on drum-and-percussion museum downtown that kids love. The Eiteljorg Museum has strong family programming around Native American and Western art. The 100 Acres art-and-nature park at Newfields is free to roam, and the downtown canal with its pedal boats is an easy, low-cost afternoon.

What is there to do in Indianapolis with a baby or toddler?

Head for the hands-on and the outdoors. The Children's Museum has a Playscape area built for toddlers and preschoolers, the Indianapolis Zoo is stroller-friendly with dolphins and animals up close, and the downtown canal is a flat, easy walk with pedal boats. Keep days short and nap-friendly, and pick a hotel with a pool to burn off energy in the evening.

How much do Indianapolis attractions cost?

The big attractions run roughly $25 to $30 per person, with the Children's Museum and zoo around $29 and others like the Indiana State Museum closer to $17. Both the museum and the zoo use dynamic pricing, so booking online a few days ahead usually costs less than paying at the gate. If you plan to visit several, compare the Indy Attraction Pass and check library museum-pass programs.

How many days do you need in Indianapolis with kids?

Two days is the sweet spot, enough for a full day at the Children's Museum plus a day downtown at the zoo, canal and a museum, without rushing. One day can work if you focus on the Children's Museum and the canal. Add a third day if you want to fit Conner Prairie, Newfields or the Motor Speedway Museum at a relaxed pace.

Is Indianapolis a good winter trip with kids?

Yes. Most of the headline attractions are indoors, so cold weather barely matters, and the city leans into the season. Newfields Winterlights turns the gardens into a walk-through holiday light display, the Children's Museum runs its Jolly Days Winter Wonderland, and downtown's Monument Circle becomes the Circle of Lights. Hotel rates are often lower in winter too, so it can be a smart time to go.
