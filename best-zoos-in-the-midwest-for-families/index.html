Best zoos in the Midwest for families

The Midwest has some of the best zoos in the country, including the one USA Today keeps ranking number one. But the picks are the easy part. The real value, the part most lists skip, is the free zoos, the reciprocal-membership trick that cuts admission across the whole region, and knowing which zoo survives a July afternoon. Here is all of it.

The short answerTL;DR

For the single best zoo day, it is Omaha's Henry Doorly Zoo, repeatedly ranked the best in the country, and almost entirely indoors, so it works in any weather. For value, nothing beats the Saint Louis Zoo: world-class and free to enter. The Milwaukee County Zoo and Brookfield Zoo Chicago are the reliable close-to-home days. But before you pay full price anywhere, read the membership hack below, because a single home-zoo membership can get your family discounted or free entry at zoos across the Midwest.

People underestimate Midwest zoos, right up until they learn that Omaha's Henry Doorly has been named the best zoo in the entire country by USA Today, more than once. A zoo is one of the most reliable days in family travel: hours of walking off energy, animals everyone remembers, and it works from toddler age up. But here is the thing nobody tells you before your first few visits, the zoos themselves are the easy decision. Where families overpay and overpack is in the stuff around the zoo: the parking, the paid add-ons, the full-price tickets you did not need to buy. So this guide does two jobs. First, the honest rundown of which zoo is worth it and for whom. Then the money hacks that change the math on all of them.

Mapped · the best zoos across the Midwest

The top pick · Worth a trip of its ownOmaha's Henry Doorly Zoo, Nebraska

If you build a weekend around one Midwest zoo, make it Omaha's Henry Doorly. It has been ranked the best in the country by USA Today 10Best in back-to-back years, and its signatures are all indoors: the Desert Dome (the world's largest indoor desert), the Lied Jungle rainforest, the Scott Aquarium with a walk-through shark tunnel, and Kingdoms of the Night, a nocturnal exhibit kids find genuinely thrilling. That indoor density is the real family advantage: it is one of the only zoos that is a great day in rain, cold or heat.

The Desert Dome at Omaha's zoo

The honest caveat: Omaha is a true destination zoo with destination pricing, and the train, the Skyfari chairlift and the feedings are paid extras on top of a not-cheap admission, which adds up fast for a family of four. It is worth it, but go in knowing it is a full-day, plan-ahead trip, not a casual stop. Arrive at opening, because the domes get warm and packed by midday.

The value champion · World-class and freeSaint Louis Zoo, Missouri

The Saint Louis Zoo in Forest Park is the one that quietly wins on value, because it is free to enter, every day, and it is not a token free zoo, it is a genuinely world-class one with more than 16,000 animals. Kids make a beeline for Sea Lion Sound, where an underwater tunnel puts sea lions swimming over your head, and the Children's Zoo. A handful of experiences (the railroad, the sea lion show, the stingray touch) are paid, and you pay to park, but the core day costs nothing. It also stacks: in St. Louis the zoo, the science center and the art museum are all free, which is why we build a whole trip around it in our things to do in St. Louis with kids guide.

The reliable classic · Easy with little kidsMilwaukee County Zoo, Wisconsin

Wide, stroller-friendly paths at the Milwaukee County Zoo

The Milwaukee County Zoo is the dependable family day: 190 wooded acres, wide paved paths that are genuinely easy with a stroller, real tree shade for hot afternoons, and enough indoor animal houses that a grey forecast barely dents the plan. It runs a handful of Family Free Days through the year when general admission is waived, which is the single biggest saving here if your dates are flexible. Save the miniature train for late in the day, right when little legs decide walking is over. Full detail in our Milwaukee County Zoo with kids guide.

The big-city pick · Near ChicagoBrookfield Zoo Chicago, Illinois

Less than 30 minutes from downtown Chicago, Brookfield Zoo is one of the country's best, with more than 3,400 animals and the small-but-crucial family touches that bigger zoos forget: playgrounds, a sensory-friendly room, a nursing room, and stroller and electric-vehicle rentals for when the acreage wins. The carousel, the dolphin show and the motor-safari are paid add-ons. If you are doing a Chicago family trip, this is the zoo to build in, and it is far calmer than trying to do Lincoln Park Zoo in the thick of the city (though Lincoln Park is free, if you want a shorter downtown stop).

Zoo plus gardens · Ohio's famous hippoCincinnati Zoo & Botanical Garden, Ohio

The Cincinnati Zoo is consistently ranked among the top two or three in the country, and it is home to Fiona the hippo, who is a genuine celebrity your kids may already know from videos. It doubles as one of the best botanical gardens in the United States, so it is unusually green and pretty for a zoo, and seasonal shows like Cheetah Encounters are included with admission. A strong pick when you want a zoo that also keeps the grown-ups happy.

For the dolphin fans · IndianaIndianapolis Zoo, Indiana

The Indianapolis Zoo is the inland zoo with dolphins, including a dolphin presentation included with admission and an underwater viewing dome, which almost no landlocked zoo can offer. Add elephants, walruses, a family roller coaster and the White River Gardens next door and it is a varied day. It pairs naturally with the Children's Museum of Indianapolis, the largest in the world, for a strong two-day Indianapolis family trip. One planning note: Indianapolis uses dynamic pricing, so tickets are cheaper booked online in advance than at the gate.

One more worth knowing in the core Midwest: the Kansas City Zoo in Missouri is a well-priced, genuinely good family day, with the Helzberg Penguin Plaza (little kids hunt for the hidden nesting boxes), a sea lion habitat and an African area. It is an easy pairing with a Kansas City trip, and a reminder that the good zoos in the Midwest are not just the famous five.

The zoo you pick barely matters. The membership you buy, and the day you go, is where families actually win or lose.

The hack most lists skip · Read this before you bookOne membership, discounted entry across the Midwest

Here is the move that changes the math on every zoo above. Most accredited US zoos take part in the AZA reciprocal admission program, which means a membership at your home zoo gets you 50 percent off, and sometimes free, admission at participating zoos across the country. For a Midwest family, that is huge: buy one membership at the zoo you visit most, and a summer road trip past two or three other zoos suddenly costs a fraction of full-price tickets, often paying the membership back in a single trip.

The rules worth knowing before you count on it: participation is optional, so not every zoo honors it, and zoos close to each other usually will not reciprocate (your local and its neighbor often exclude one another). You must show your physical home membership, the discount covers general admission on normal days (special events and the paid add-ons are excluded), and there is no limit on visits. Because each zoo sets its own policy, the one habit that saves a bad surprise at the gate is to check the two zoos' reciprocity before you drive. Done right, this is the single biggest money-saver in this whole guide.

Field note

Quick math: if a family of four pays full gate price at three zoos on a summer trip, a single home-zoo family membership often costs less than those three days combined, and then gets you 50 percent off (or free) at the other two, plus free admission all year at your home zoo. If you visit more than one zoo a year, a membership almost always wins. Buy it at the zoo you go to most, not the cheapest one.

The other angle · December is the secret seasonMidwest zoos are at their best in the cold

A zoo holiday light tunnel in December

Here is a season families overlook: in December, Midwest zoos turn into some of the best holiday-lights events in the country, and a few are included with normal admission, which is rare. Cincinnati's PNC Festival of Lights (around four million lights, with the animals in heated buildings) and Columbus Wildlights are both included with admission. Indianapolis' Christmas at the Zoo has the distinction of being the first zoo holiday-lights event in the nation, running since 1967, with Santa and reindeer. Saint Louis' U.S. Bank Wild Lights and Brookfield's Holiday Magic (a 600-foot light tunnel and a walk-through gingerbread house) are ticketed separately, so buy early. And the Milwaukee County Zoo runs a one-mile Wild Lights path with some animal buildings open at night.

Why it is worth it with kids: the crowds are thinner than summer, the heat problem disappears, and a zoo lit up at night is genuinely magical for small children in a way a July walk is not. Dress for real cold, go early in the evening before bedtime melts everyone, and book the ticketed events in advance, because the good nights sell out.

And the opposite problem · SummerHow to survive a July zoo day

Midsummer is when a zoo day goes wrong: it is hot, the animals hide, and everyone wilts by lunch. Three fixes. Go at opening, when animals are active and the heat is bearable, and be prepared to leave by early afternoon. Lean on the indoor zoos on the worst days, Omaha's domes and aquarium are the gold standard, and most big zoos have air-conditioned animal houses to cycle through. And chase the water: several zoos have splash areas, and just south of the Midwest the Louisville Zoo's Glacier Run splash park is included with admission, which turns a brutal hot day into a water day at no extra cost (see our Louisville Zoo with kids guide).

The zoo-day money checklist
  • Check free days first. Saint Louis is free daily; Milwaukee and others run Family Free Days. Build the trip around them if your dates flex.
  • Buy one reciprocal membership at the zoo you visit most, then use it for 50 percent off (or free) at participating zoos across the region.
  • Book online in advance at zoos with dynamic pricing (Indianapolis and others), where gate prices run higher.
  • Pack your own water and snacks. Most zoos allow it, and in-park food for four adds up faster than admission.
  • Decide the paid add-ons before you go in, not in the moment: pick one ride or feeding, saved for the afternoon, and skip the rest.
  • Factor parking. Even free-entry zoos (Saint Louis) charge to park, so it is a real line in the budget.
From our family · what actually works

Two rules make a zoo day with little kids go smoothly. Go at opening, because the animals are liveliest, the heat is kinder, and you beat the stroller traffic. And save the one paid ride or feeding you have agreed on for the back half of the day, right when legs give out and morale dips, so it buys you another hour instead of being over by ten in the morning.

Admission prices, free-day dates, reciprocity policies, show schedules, ride fees and holiday-event tickets all change by season, and some exhibits close for renovation. Confirm the current details and the two zoos' reciprocal policies on each official site before you go.

Before you go

What is the best zoo in the Midwest?

Omaha's Henry Doorly Zoo in Nebraska is the most awarded, ranked the best in the United States by USA Today 10Best in back-to-back years, and it is largely indoors (the Desert Dome, Lied Jungle rainforest and a shark-tunnel aquarium), so it works in any weather. For value, the Saint Louis Zoo is world-class and free to enter. Brookfield near Chicago and the Milwaukee County Zoo are the best reliable close-to-home family days.

Which Midwest zoos are free?

The Saint Louis Zoo is free to enter every day (you pay only for parking and a few paid experiences inside), and Lincoln Park Zoo in Chicago is also free. Most other big Midwest zoos are not free daily but run Family Free Days through the year when general admission is waived, so check each zoo's calendar before you plan.

How does the zoo reciprocal membership discount work?

Most accredited US zoos take part in the AZA reciprocal admission program, where a membership at your home zoo earns 50 percent off, and sometimes free, admission at other participating zoos. It is optional, so not every zoo honors it, and zoos close to each other usually exclude one another. You must show your physical membership, and special events and paid add-ons are not covered. For a family visiting more than one zoo a year, it usually pays for itself quickly.

Which Midwest zoos have holiday lights?

Nearly all the big ones. Cincinnati's PNC Festival of Lights and Columbus Wildlights are included with admission; Indianapolis' Christmas at the Zoo is the oldest in the nation (since 1967); and Saint Louis' Wild Lights and Brookfield's Holiday Magic are ticketed separately, so book early. December crowds are thinner than summer and a lit-up zoo is magical for little kids, so it is an underrated time to go.

What is the best Midwest zoo for toddlers?

The Milwaukee County Zoo and the Saint Louis Zoo are both excellent with toddlers: wide paved paths, easy stroller access, shade and indoor animal houses for bad weather. Go at opening when animals are active and crowds are thin, pack your own snacks, and save one paid train or carousel ride for the afternoon when little legs tire.
