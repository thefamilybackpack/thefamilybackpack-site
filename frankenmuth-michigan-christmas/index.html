Frankenmuth, Michigan · Christmas

Frankenmuth at Christmas, with kids

Michigan's Little Bavaria runs on Christmas all year round, and from late November it turns the whole town into one. Here is what is actually worth your time with kids: the world's largest Christmas store, the lights and the carriage rides, the famous family-style chicken dinners, and where to stay so the day can end in an indoor waterpark.

Frankenmuth is a small Bavarian-themed town in the middle of Michigan that decided, decades ago, to be about Christmas, and then committed completely. At its heart is Bronner's, a Christmas store the size of one and a half football fields, open nearly every day of the year. Around it sits a walkable village of German bakeries, fudge shops, a wooden covered bridge and two enormous chicken-dinner halls. For about five weeks from late November it all lights up, and the combination of a free indoor wonderland, a decorated town, and waterpark hotels to crash in makes it one of the easiest Christmas trips in the Midwest to do with young kids. Here is how to get the most out of it, and what to skip.

First things firstWhere is Frankenmuth, and how do you get there?

Frankenmuth sits in the middle of Michigan's Lower Peninsula, in Saginaw County, about 90 minutes north of Detroit straight up I-75, roughly two hours from Grand Rapids and Lansing, and about four and a half hours from Chicago. That puts it in easy weekend-trip range for most of the eastern Midwest. The town itself is tiny and walkable: almost everything that matters, Bronner's, the two chicken halls, the covered bridge and the shops, is within a mile or so, though you will want the car to hop between Bronner's (just south of the center on Christmas Lane) and Main Street. In December, give yourself extra time: the one main road through town backs up on weekends, and parking near Main Street fills by midday.

The main eventBronner's CHRISTmas Wonderland

If you do one thing in Frankenmuth, it is Bronner's, and the good news is it costs nothing to walk in. Billed as the world's largest Christmas store, it is about the size of one and a half football fields under one roof, stocked with more than 50,000 ornaments, trims and gifts, and it is open 361 days a year with free admission and free parking. For kids it lands somewhere between a toy store and a theme park: thousands of lit trees, animated displays, a whole aisle of ornaments from every country, and a Santa to meet during the season. Outside, the Silent Night Memorial Chapel (a replica of the Austrian chapel where the carol was written) is open year-round, and Christmas Lane is lit every night, which is a free after-dark drive-through even in July.

A few practical things with children. Bronner's is big, so grab a map at the door and decide in advance whether you are browsing or buying, because a toddler's patience will not survive all 98,000 square feet. The aisles are wide and stroller-friendly, there are restrooms and a snack area, and the fragile ornaments are mostly up high, but keep little hands busy. The single busiest time is weekend afternoons from Thanksgiving to Christmas; if you can come on a weekday or right at opening, you will have room to actually enjoy it. Check bronners.com/hours before you go, since the store runs extended evening hours in the Christmas season and shorter ones in winter and spring.

The town after darkThe lights, the carriage rides and the Glockenspiel

Frankenmuth leans hard into its Bavarian looks, and at Christmas the whole of Main Street is strung with lights, wreaths and garland, which makes an evening stroll the best free thing in town after Bronner's. The signature splurge is a horse-drawn carriage ride through the lit streets, a short, genuinely lovely outing that small kids love (dress warm, it is open-air). Watch and listen for the Glockenspiel, the 35-bell carillon tower on the Bavarian Inn, which plays several times a day and sends carved wooden figures out to act the story of the Pied Piper of Hamelin, a free few minutes of magic if you time it. The wooden covered bridge over the Cass River is the other classic photo stop, especially with snow on it.

Across the river, the Frankenmuth River Place Shops is an open-air shopping village built for wandering, with family attractions tucked in (a mirror maze, a rope course and more) and, in season, Santa visits on weekends. It is a good rainy-or-freezing-afternoon backup when everyone has had enough of the cold. None of the town's Christmas-season extras run on a fixed festival schedule you can count on in 2026, so treat the lights, the carriage rides and the Glockenspiel as the reliable core and anything else as a bonus.

Beyond the shoppingMore for the kids, the gnome hunt, the rink and the farm

Frankenmuth has a handful of extras that are built for children, and they stretch a Christmas visit past the shops and dinners. The best free one is the Frankenmuth Gnome Hunt, a self-guided scavenger hunt with more than 40 gnomes hidden in businesses around town; you pick up a map (or scan a QR code) at the Visitor Center, track down the gnomes, and redeem a small prize once you have found fifteen. It is free, it keeps kids engaged as you wander, and it works in any weather you can walk in. In winter, the town sets up the Frankenmuth Ice Rink, a seasonal chilled outdoor rink with skate rentals and a warming tent, which is a fun hour between shops (confirm the current season dates before you count on it). And just outside the center, Grandpa Tiny's Farm, a historic petting farm, runs a seasonal Santa's Reindeer Farm at Christmas, with animals to meet and Santa on site; its dates change each year, so check ahead. None of these are must-dos the way Bronner's is, but any one of them turns a half-day into a full one for younger kids.

The famous dinnersZehnder's vs the Bavarian Inn, the chicken question

Frankenmuth is almost as famous for fried chicken as for Christmas. Two giant family-style restaurants face each other across Main Street, each serving an all-you-can-eat chicken dinner brought to the table in platters, with the sides (buttered noodles, mashed potatoes, stuffing, coleslaw, warm bread) included. Zehnder's is the larger of the two and serves on the order of a million guests a year; the Bavarian Inn, the one with the Glockenspiel tower, leans harder into the German theme. Honestly, for most families the chicken is very similar and either is a safe bet, so choose on logistics: the Bavarian Inn if you are staying at its Lodge or want the German atmosphere, Zehnder's if you want the shortest wait or are tied to Splash Village. Both take reservations, and in December you absolutely want one, because walk-in waits on a holiday weekend can run very long.

From our family · the chicken halls with kids

Two things we learned eating here with children. First, these are big, loud, cheerful rooms, not fussy restaurants, so a wiggly toddler is completely at home and nobody blinks. Second, the portions are enormous and it is family-style, so you do not need to over-order; a couple of kids can basically share off the adult platters, which makes it better value than the menu suggests. We booked a reservation for a late-afternoon dinner, before the evening rush, and walked straight in while the standby line stretched out the door.

Where to sleepThe waterpark-hotel move

This is the detail that turns a nice day trip into a proper family weekend: Frankenmuth has indoor-waterpark hotels, so the kids can do Christmas shopping and a decorated town all day and still cannonball into a pool at night. The big one is Zehnder's Splash Village Hotel and Waterpark, billed as Michigan's largest indoor waterpark, which means slides, a lazy river, a toddler area and warm water whatever the weather outside. The Bavarian Inn Lodge is the other family heavyweight, a huge Bavarian-themed hotel on the river with indoor pools, a family fun center and an arcade, and it is walkable to the Glockenspiel and Main Street. Either one lets you build the trip the way the Wisconsin Dells works, around the hotel, with the town as the daytime outing. Book early for December weekends, when both fill up.

If the indoor-waterpark idea is really what you are after, it is worth knowing Frankenmuth is one of several Midwest towns that do it well. For how it compares, and the bigger resorts a few hours west, see our guides to the best Midwest indoor waterparks and the best Midwest family resorts.

Timing it rightWhen to go in 2026, and how to beat the crowds

Frankenmuth is a Christmas town every day of the year thanks to Bronner's, but the town itself feels most festive from the weekend before Thanksgiving through the New Year, once the lights are up. That window is also the busiest, and the crowds are real: the single main road clogs, Bronner's parking lot fills, and the chicken halls run long waits. The fix is simple if you have any flexibility. Come on a weekday in early-to-mid December, when the town is fully decorated but the weekend crush has not arrived, arrive at Bronner's near opening, and lock in a dinner reservation. A light dusting of snow makes the whole place, so if you can watch the forecast and move, do. Avoid the Saturdays right before Christmas unless crowds are part of the fun for you.

Putting it togetherA simple one-night Frankenmuth Christmas plan

Here is the shape of a trip that works with kids. Afternoon one: check into the waterpark hotel, drop the bags, and head to Bronner's before it gets dark so the kids get the full wonderland while they are fresh. Evening one: an early family-style chicken dinner (reserved), then a slow walk down the lit Main Street and a carriage ride if the budget allows, back to the hotel for the waterpark. Morning two: a German bakery breakfast, the covered bridge and the River Place Shops, catch the Glockenspiel, and drive home through Christmas Lane's lights on the way out. One night is plenty for the essentials; a second night only if you want a slow pace or a full day in the water.

Before you go

Is Frankenmuth worth visiting at Christmas with kids?

Yes, it is one of the easiest Christmas trips in the Midwest to do with young children. Bronner's, the world's largest Christmas store, is free to walk into and genuinely wows kids, the town is decorated and lit from late November, and the indoor-waterpark hotels mean the day can end in the pool. Add the family-style chicken dinners and it is a very low-stress, high-magic overnight.

Is Bronner's CHRISTmas Wonderland free?

Yes. Bronner's has free admission and free parking, and it is open 361 days a year. It is the size of about one and a half football fields, with more than 50,000 ornaments, trims and gifts, plus the year-round Silent Night Memorial Chapel outside and Christmas Lane lights that run nightly. You only pay if you buy something.

When is the best time to visit Frankenmuth for Christmas?

The town feels most festive from the weekend before Thanksgiving through the New Year, once the lights are up. For families, a weekday in early-to-mid December is the sweet spot, fully decorated but without the heavy weekend crowds. The Saturdays right before Christmas are the busiest, with long waits and packed parking, so go midweek or arrive early if you can.

Is the Frankenmuth ChristKindlMarkt happening in 2026?

No. The Frankenmuth ChristKindlMarkt is not being held in 2026; the organizers have said they are using the year to plan and that a market may return in the future. Do not build your trip around a Christmas market. The reliable Christmas draws are Bronner's, the Main Street lights, carriage rides and the Glockenspiel, all of which run without it.

What free things are there to do in Frankenmuth with kids?

Quite a lot. Walking into Bronner's, the world's largest Christmas store, is free, as is its nightly Christmas Lane light display and the Silent Night Memorial Chapel. Strolling the lit Main Street, watching the Glockenspiel tower show on the Bavarian Inn, and the covered bridge are all free too. The town also runs a free, self-guided Gnome Hunt scavenger hunt (pick up a map at the Visitor Center), which keeps kids busy as you wander. You only really pay for the carriage rides, the ice rink, meals and anything you buy.

Where should families stay in Frankenmuth?

The two family favorites are both indoor-waterpark or pool hotels. Zehnder's Splash Village Hotel and Waterpark is billed as Michigan's largest indoor waterpark, and the Bavarian Inn Lodge is a large riverside hotel with indoor pools, an arcade and a family fun center, walkable to Main Street. Staying at either lets you run the trip around the hotel, with the town as the daytime outing. Book early for December weekends.

What is the difference between Zehnder's and the Bavarian Inn?

They are the two big family-style chicken-dinner restaurants, facing each other on Main Street, and both serve an all-you-can-eat fried chicken dinner with sides. Zehnder's is the larger and serves about a million guests a year, while the Bavarian Inn, the one with the Glockenspiel tower, leans harder into the German theme. For most families the food is similar, so pick on logistics, your hotel, the wait, or which atmosphere you prefer. Reserve ahead in December.

How far is Frankenmuth from Detroit and Chicago?

Frankenmuth is about 90 minutes north of Detroit straight up I-75, roughly two hours from Grand Rapids and Lansing, and about four and a half hours from Chicago. That makes it an easy weekend trip from most of the eastern Midwest. The town is small and walkable once you arrive, though you will want the car to move between Bronner's and Main Street.

How long do you need in Frankenmuth at Christmas?

One night covers the essentials comfortably, with time for Bronner's, a family-style dinner, the lights and a carriage ride, plus a spell in the hotel waterpark. Add a second night only if you want a slower pace or a full day in the water. A day trip is doable if you live within a couple of hours, but the overnight is what makes it feel like a real holiday outing for kids.
