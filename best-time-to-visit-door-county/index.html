Door County, Wisconsin · When to Go

Best time to visit Door County, with kids

Door County is a different trip in every season, and the "best" time really depends on what your family wants: warm bay swimming, cherry picking, peak fall color, or the cheapest and quietest week on the peninsula. Here is a month-by-month guide, with the honest answers on weather, crowds and price.

Summer on the Door County bay
The short answerTL;DR

For most families, the sweet spot is mid-July to late August: the bay is warm enough to swim, cherry picking is on, and everything is open, though it is also the busiest and priciest stretch. Want fewer crowds? Early-to-mid September keeps the warm water and loses the July crowds. For peak fall color, aim for early to mid October (but book around the festival weekends). The cheapest and least crowded months are November through March, when the peninsula is quiet and cold and many shops and restaurants close. The one thing to time carefully: specific weekends in October can be as busy as July.

There is no single best time to visit Door County, there is the best time for the trip you want. The peninsula runs on seasons more than almost any family destination in the Midwest: the same harbor town that is packed and sun-soaked in July is boarded up and silent in January. Summer is for the water and the cherries, fall is for the color and the festivals, winter is cheap and quiet, and spring is the shoulder nobody talks about. Pick what matters most to your family, then use the month-by-month guide below to land on a week.

Field note

The crowd surprise most families miss: October weekends can be as busy as July. Peak fall color collides with two big festivals, the Egg Harbor Pumpkin Patch (around October 10 to 11 in 2026) and Sister Bay Fall Fest (around October 16 to 18 in 2026), and lodging fills for miles. If you want the color without the crush, go on an October weekday, and book very early if you want a festival weekend.

The season breakdownSummer, for swimming and cherries

July and August are the headline months, and for good reason. It is the only stretch when the bay water is reliably warm enough to swim, which for families with kids is the whole point of a Door County beach day (stick to the calmer, shallower Green Bay side, see our family beaches guide). It is also cherry season: sweet cherries in early July, tart Montmorency through mid-July into early August, so you can pair a beach morning with an afternoon of pick-your-own at an orchard. Everything is open, hours are long, and the festivals roll. The tradeoff is simple: this is the busiest and most expensive time, and you need to book lodging months ahead.

The quiet sweet spotSeptember, the shoulder that works

If we had to pick one month for families who want the best of summer without the peak, it is September. The water is still warm from a summer of sun, daytime highs sit in the comfortable low 70s, the crowds thin out as schools go back, and prices ease off the July ceiling. You lose some of the long summer hours and the cherry season is over, but you gain breathing room and better lodging deals. Early September in particular is a genuinely underrated time to bring kids to the peninsula.

The color showOctober, for peak fall

Door County puts on one of the Midwest's best fall color displays, and it peaks early to mid October (color starts up north and inland and moves south). This is a gorgeous family trip, orchard markets, pumpkin patches, scenic drives under the canopy, see our fall in Door County guide, but it comes with the crowd warning above: the festival weekends are as packed as summer. Go on a weekday for color without chaos, and know that by late October many leaves are down and seasonal businesses start trimming hours.

Cheap and quietWinter and spring, the off-season

November through March is the cheapest and least crowded time in Door County, by a wide margin. It is also cold (highs in the 20s to low 40s) and much of the peninsula shuts down, so set expectations: this is a trip for a cozy cabin, a snowy walk, and events like the Fish Creek Winter Festival in early February, not for a full slate of open attractions. Spring (April and May) is the thaw: things reopen gradually, the cherry and apple blossoms bloom around mid-to-late May, and you get low-season prices with milder weather, though the water is far too cold to swim and some businesses wait until Memorial Day to open fully.

From our family · how we choose

With kids, we match the month to the kids we have that year. When they were little and all they wanted was shallow water and sand, we went in late July for the warm bay, crowds and all. As they got older and the swimming mattered less, we shifted to September for the quiet, and once to early October for the color, on a weekday, which we would do again. The one season we would skip with young kids is deep winter, not because it is bad, but because so little is open that a rainy-equivalent day has nowhere to go.

Quick answersCheapest, least crowded and busiest

The short version for planners. Cheapest: November through March, when demand bottoms out (accept that much is closed). Least crowded: the same off-season months, plus September weekdays. Busiest to avoid if crowds stress you: July and August, and the October festival weekends. Best weather: July and August for warmth (upper 70s), with May and September pleasant in the 60s. Best all-round family month: late August into September, for warm water and thinning crowds, or mid-July if cherry picking and peak-summer energy are the priority.

Before you go

What is the best time to visit Door County with kids?

For most families, mid-July to late August, when the bay is warm enough to swim, cherry picking is on, and everything is open, though it is the busiest and priciest time. For warm water with fewer crowds, early-to-mid September is the sweet spot. For peak fall color, go early to mid October but avoid the festival weekends unless you book ahead.

What is the cheapest time to visit Door County?

November through March, by a wide margin, when tourism bottoms out and lodging is at its lowest. The tradeoff is cold weather (highs in the 20s to low 40s) and many shops, restaurants and attractions closed for the season. For cheap-but-milder, mid-to-late May offers low-season prices as businesses reopen.

When is the least crowded time in Door County?

The off-season from November through March is quietest, followed by weekdays in spring and September. The busiest times are July and August and, surprisingly, the October festival weekends (Egg Harbor Pumpkin Patch and Sister Bay Fall Fest), when peak color and events fill lodging for miles.

When can you swim in Door County?

Realistically July and August, when the bay water is warm enough for kids. Stick to the Green Bay side, which is calmer, shallower and warmer than the open Lake Michigan side. September can still be swimmable after a warm summer, but spring and early summer water is cold.

When is peak fall color in Door County?

Usually early to mid October, with color starting up north and inland and moving south. Many leaves are down by late October. The color weekends coincide with big festivals, so go on a weekday for the views without the crowds, and check the Travel Wisconsin fall color report for the current year's timing.

When is cherry picking season in Door County?

Roughly early July through mid-August. Sweet cherries ripen first in early July, then the tart Montmorency cherries, the main u-pick crop, run from mid-July into early August. See our Door County cherry picking guide for the orchards and current timing.

Is Door County worth visiting in winter?

It can be, for a quiet, cozy, cheap getaway with snow and events like the Fish Creek Winter Festival in early February, but set expectations, it is cold and much of the peninsula closes for the season. With young kids who need lots of open attractions, winter is the hardest season to fill; summer and fall are easier.
