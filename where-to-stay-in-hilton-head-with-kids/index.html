Hilton Head Island · With Kids

Where to stay in Hilton Head with kids, area by area

We know this island well, well enough to stop thinking of it as one place. To a family, Hilton Head is really five or six different vacations stacked along twelve miles of sand, and the one you get depends almost entirely on which part you book. Here is how we would choose, the way we actually think about it after coming back year after year.

The short answerTL;DR

Pick your area first, then the resort. First time, want to walk to the beach and dinner: Coligny and Forest Beach. Classic family-resort week, quiet beach, pools and bike paths: Palmetto Dunes. The prettiest, most self-contained bubble (if the budget allows): Sea Pines. Quieter, more local, better value: Port Royal and Mid-Island. Traveling with a toddler: head for the widest, calmest sand and a resort with a zero-entry pool. Get the area right and you barely touch the car all week.

The decisionThe areas, compared

Hilton Head is shaped like a shoe, and the "plantations" (the island's word for its big gated communities) line the sole along the beach. Each one is really its own little resort town. This is the table we wish someone had handed us the first time.

The south endSea Pines

Best for: families who want everything inside one gate. Sea Pines is the big forested preserve at the south end, the part most people picture when they picture Hilton Head: the candy-striped Harbour Town lighthouse, miles of bike paths under the live oaks, and a wide, firm beach you can walk for an hour. For a family it is about as easy as the island gets. Almost everything you need is inside the gate, so once you are in you barely touch the car. The trade-offs are honest ones: it is the priciest corner of the island, and day visitors pay a gate pass. With little kids we steer toward the quieter south end near South Beach, where the water on the Calibogue Sound side is calmer and shallower than the open Atlantic. Sea Pines also hides a real bonus for young families: Lawton Stables in the Forest Preserve, with pony rides and a small animal farm (goats, pot-bellied pigs, miniature donkeys and alpacas) that toddlers adore. Is it worth the premium? If you want the prettiest, most self-contained week and the budget allows, yes; if you are mostly after beach and value, you can do better elsewhere.

The family classicPalmetto Dunes

Best for: a classic resort week with a calm beach. If one area is purpose-built for families, it is Palmetto Dunes: a gated, resort-style stretch mid-island with a notably calm, gently shelving beach, big resort pools, a lagoon you can kayak, tennis and pickleball, and some of the island's better kids programs. It is less walkable than Coligny, so expect to bike or shuttle to dinner, but for a park-the-car-and-relax week with young kids it is our default. The two oceanfront anchors are the Omni Hilton Head Oceanfront Resort and the Hilton Beachfront Resort and Spa (the former Marriott), side by side right on the sand with big multi-pool decks. One note for toddler parents: the Omni's Kids Camp starts at age 4, so for under-fours the draw is the pools and the calm beach rather than a program. Villas and rental homes across Palmetto Dunes, many with kitchens, are often the better-value call for a longer stay.

The walkable oneForest Beach & Coligny

Best for: a first trip, or anyone who wants to leave the car alone. This is the one corner where you can walk from your room to the sand, to dinner, and to ice cream. Coligny Beach is the island's most public, amenity-rich beach (a splash fountain, showers, chairs, lifeguards in season), and Coligny Plaza packs shops and casual restaurants a block back. The trade-off is that it is the busiest, liveliest part of the island, which is exactly what some families want and others come to escape. Coligny Beach Park itself is genuinely great with kids: a musical splash fountain, showers, chairs and lifeguards in season. On the sand, South Forest Beach is the better base if you want to walk to Coligny's shops and restaurants, while North Forest Beach is quieter and more residential, mostly rental homes, and still an easy ride to the action.

The quiet middleShipyard, Port Royal & Mid-Island

Shipyard sits right next to Coligny but behind a gate, so you get a calm beach and quick access to the Coligny action without staying in the thick of it. Port Royal, at the north end, is quieter and more spacious, with wide beaches and family condos, though you will drive to most restaurants. And Mid-Island (the non-gated stretch along the main road) is where the value is: condos and rentals a short drive from the beach, ideal for a longer stay or a bigger group watching the budget. In Port Royal, the Westin is the family anchor: three pools, a quiet beach, and cribs as standard, which matters with a toddler. Mid-island, the public beach parks at Driessen Beach and Folly Field have parking, showers and easy access, so a condo nearby can be smart value. The thing to know: mid-island is not gated and not beachfront, so you trade a little polish and a short beach drive for real savings.

The picksBest resorts for families

Once you have your area, the resort is easier. A few that families come back to: the Palmetto Dunes resorts (villas with kitchens, big pools, the lagoon and kids programs), the Sea Pines rentals and the Harbour Town side for the all-in-one bubble, and Disney's Hilton Head Island Resort near Shelter Cove for Disney families (a short shuttle from the beach). The island's real strength for families, though, is vacation rentals and villas: a two or three bedroom with a kitchen and a washer beats a hotel room once you are a family of four, and often costs less per night. We would compare live rates across a few before booking. The short list we would actually point a friend to: the Omni or the Hilton Beachfront in Palmetto Dunes for oceanfront pools, the Westin in Port Royal for a quieter beach and toddler-ready rooms, Sea Pines villas for space and a kitchen, Sonesta in Shipyard for a big pool a short walk from the sand, and Disney's Hilton Head Island Resort for Disney families. The honest trade-offs: Disney's beach is a shuttle ride rather than a walk, and Sonesta sits a short walk back from the beach rather than on it.

One option most family guides miss: Hilton Head also has upscale RV and motorcoach resorts. If you travel in an RV, or want a different kind of stay for a longer trip, they can be excellent value and very family-friendly.

Local note

A thing to know before you book a ground-floor villa: Hilton Head is laced with freshwater lagoons, and they do have alligators. They mostly keep to themselves, but the rule we give every family is simple: stay back from lagoon edges, never let kids or dogs play near the water, and never feed them. The ocean beaches are the opposite, wide, flat and calm, which is exactly why they are so good with little ones.

What to actually doThings to do with kids

The island is built for easy, low-key family days. A few that earn their place:

  • Bike everywhere. Hilton Head's flat, paved pathway network is the signature thing to do. Rent bikes (many rentals deliver to your door with kid seats and trailers) and the whole island opens up without the car. Riding the hard-packed beach at low tide is a rite of passage.
  • Hunt for shark teeth. The beaches here are full of fossilized shark teeth, especially at low tide and after a storm. It is free, it is addictive, and it keeps kids busy for hours. The trick: go at low tide and scan the dark shell beds right at the waterline and along the last high-tide line, looking for small shiny black or gray triangles. They turn up all over the island, with the quieter south-end beaches and the days right after a storm the most reliable.
  • Harbour Town. Climb the red-and-white lighthouse, find the bronze Neptune statue over the yacht basin, and in summer catch the live music under the Liberty Oak (a decades-long island tradition kids love). Shops and restaurants ring the marina.
  • Lawton Stables, in the Sea Pines Forest Preserve. Shetland pony rides for little ones, trail rides for ages 8 and up, and a small animal farm with goats, pot-bellied pigs, miniature donkeys and alpacas. A toddler favorite, and one of the island's quiet gems.
  • Shelter Cove HarbourFest. From June through August the harbour runs nightly entertainment (the longtime island performer Shannon Tanner plays twice a night, plus face-painting and balloon artists for the little ones) and fireworks on Tuesday nights over the marina, alongside shops and easy dinners. Check the current season's calendar before you go.
  • The Salty Dog Cafe at South Beach Marina in Sea Pines: a Hilton Head institution with casual food, live music, and the famous mascot and t-shirts. A go-once family evening.
  • Mini golf and the arcade. Adventure Cove runs two themed 18-hole courses and the island's largest arcade, which doubles as a rainy-day option.
  • On the water. Dolphin cruises, pirate-ship tours and easy kayak trips through the lagoons and salt marsh, all gentle enough for young kids. Older kids can zipline over Broad Creek.
  • Fishing and crabbing. Drop a line off a dock or try crabbing off the docks, the cheapest, stickiest fun on the island.

When it rainsRainy day and indoor

Weather is rarely a washout here, but when a storm rolls through, the island has real indoor options, which is more than most beach towns can say:

  • The Sandbox, an interactive children's museum near Coligny: hands-on exhibits built for toddlers through early elementary. The island's best rainy-day move with little kids.
  • Coastal Discovery Museum at Honey Horn: indoor exhibits plus a 68-acre property with gardens, trails and animals, and free grounds admission. Good in any weather.
  • Adventure Cove's arcade, if you just need to burn an hour out of the rain.

Day tripsBeyond the island

Part of what makes Hilton Head a great family base is what is a short drive away:

  • Pinckney Island National Wildlife Refuge (minutes away, on the way onto the island): a free, flat, walkable and bikeable refuge full of birds, deer and, yes, alligators. A great easy-nature morning.
  • Daufuskie Island: a quaint, historic island with no cars, reached by ferry and explored by golf cart. A memorable half-day adventure.
  • Bluffton (about 15 minutes): a small, charming historic town on the May River, with galleries, easy restaurants and a slower pace.
  • Hunting Island State Park (about an hour, past Beaufort): the Lowcountry's famous lighthouse you can climb, a wild driftwood beach, and some of the best shark-teeth hunting in the state.
  • Savannah, Georgia (under an hour): the squares, the riverfront and a completely different kind of day out.

TimingWhen to go

Our favorite windows are late April through June and September into October: warm water, lighter crowds and better rates than peak July and August. Spring break (March and early April) is lovely for a calmer, family-friendly break, but the Atlantic is still cold, so plan on beach play, bikes, heated resort pools and day trips rather than long ocean swims. For dinner out, the island's restaurants spread across Coligny, Harbour Town, Shelter Cove and the local "Barmuda Triangle" cluster (good food, and the island's bar scene, so more of a parents-dinner spot than a kids hangout).

Before you go

Is Hilton Head a good place for kids?

Yes. Hilton Head is one of the easiest beach trips on the East Coast for families: wide, firm, gently sloping beaches that are good for little kids, a huge network of flat paved bike paths that let you leave the car parked, and resorts built around pools, kids camps and family villas. Pick the right area and the week more or less runs itself.

What is the best area to stay in Hilton Head with kids?

For a first trip where you want to walk to the beach and dinner, Coligny and Forest Beach. For a classic resort week with a quiet beach, pools and bike paths, Palmetto Dunes. For the prettiest, most self-contained bubble, Sea Pines. For a quieter, better-value base, Port Royal or Mid-Island.

Which beach in Hilton Head is best for families?

The island's beaches are broad, flat and firm, so most are good for young kids. Coligny Beach is the most walkable and has a splash fountain and amenities, the beaches off Palmetto Dunes and Sea Pines are quieter and calmer, and the sand gets widest at low tide, which is also when kids find the most shells and shark teeth.

What is the best month to visit Hilton Head?

Late April through June and September into October are the sweet spots: warm water, fewer crowds and lower rates than peak summer. Spring break (March and early April) can be lovely but the ocean is still cool, so it is better for the beach, bikes and pools than for long swims.

Is Hilton Head warm enough for spring break?

Usually mild and pleasant in daytime, often in the 60s and 70s in March and April, but the Atlantic is still cold, so plan on beach play, heated resort pools, bikes and day trips rather than ocean swimming. It is a calmer, more family-friendly spring break than the party beaches further south.

What are the best family resorts in Hilton Head?

The strongest family picks cluster in Palmetto Dunes and Sea Pines (resort villas, pools and kids programs), plus Disney's Hilton Head Island Resort near Shelter Cove for Disney families. Beyond hotels, Hilton Head has a deep bench of family vacation rentals and even upscale RV and motorcoach resorts, which can be better value for a longer family stay.
