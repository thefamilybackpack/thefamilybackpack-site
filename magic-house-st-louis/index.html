St. Louis, Missouri · Children's Museum

The Magic House, a parent's guide

The Magic House is St. Louis's beloved children's museum, three-plus floors of hands-on, climb-in, build-it play that can genuinely fill a day. It is also where families overpay and overpack without a plan. Here is how to do it right: the real prices, the money hacks, and which exhibits suit which age.

The Magic House in Kirkwood
The short answerTL;DR

The Magic House is the best rainy-day or any-day pick for families with kids roughly 1 to 10 in St. Louis, and it is in Kirkwood, a southwest suburb, not downtown. All-day admission recently ran about $15 (under 1 free), and parking is free. Two money moves change the math: local residents pay just $3 a person from 3 to 5pm, and the museum is free on Free Family Nights (the third Friday of the month, 5 to 8pm) for eligible counties. Plan on a half to full day, go on a weekday morning or late afternoon to dodge crowds, and keep the separate MADE for Kids makerspace (in the Delmar Loop) for the school-age crowd.

Ask any St. Louis parent for the one indoor place that never fails and they will say the Magic House. It opened in a Victorian mansion in 1979 and has grown into a 55,000-square-foot children's museum where almost everything is meant to be touched, climbed, built or slid down. It is the kind of place a three-year-old and a nine-year-old can both lose a day in, which is exactly why it works for families with a spread of ages. The trick is not deciding whether to go, it is going smart: the admission is reasonable but the extras and the crowds add up, and a little planning turns a good day into a great one.

Field note

The single best-value move: if you live in St. Louis County, the City of St. Louis, Jefferson, Franklin, St. Charles, Warren or Lincoln county, show ID and pay just $3 a person from 3 to 5pm, or go free on a Free Family Night (the third Friday, 5 to 8pm). Two hours in the late afternoon is plenty for younger kids, who fade before closing anyway. Visiting from out of town? You pay full admission, but late afternoon is still the quietest window, so aim for it.

Before you go · The money and the planPrices, hours and how long to spend

All-day admission has recently run about $15 a person for ages 1 and up, with under 1 free and free parking included. Prices creep up, so confirm the current rate before you go. On hours, the one that catches families out is that the Magic House is closed Mondays during the school year (it opens daily in summer), typically running from the morning to 5pm, with slightly earlier Saturday opening. Build your visit around two free daily extras the kids love: Story Time at 11am and 2pm and a Science Demo at 1pm.

How long should you budget? This is a place that can easily fill a full day, but with little kids a half day (three to four hours) hits the sweet spot before meltdowns. If you only have an afternoon, the 3pm resident deal makes it cheap and the crowds thin out. Weekday mornings and late afternoons are calmest; weekend midday is the overflow-lot crush.

What is insideThe best exhibits, by age

The Magic House is built in zones, and knowing which suit your kids saves a lot of back-and-forth. For babies and toddlers, head straight to Baby & Me, a dedicated soft playroom, and Wonder Works, where preschoolers push buttons and pull levers on simple machines. For preschool and up, the Children's Village is the star: a kid-sized town where they role-play as a doctor, vet, banker or shopkeeper. The Make-It Workshop and the STEAM Center (a science lab and art studio) land best with school-age kids who want to build and tinker.

The crowd-pleasers cut across ages: the giant three-story slide that drops through the center of the building, the static-electricity ball that makes hair stand on end, a replica Oval Office, and sports zones tied to the local Blues and City SC teams. A good loop with mixed ages: start high and slide down, do the Children's Village together, split for Baby & Me or the STEAM Center by age, and regroup for the 1pm Science Demo.

From our family · visiting with a toddler

With a little one, the Magic House earns its reputation on one thing: everything is meant to be handled, so nobody is shushing you. We look for a soft space to put a crawler down, somewhere to sit and feed, and enough going on that an older sibling stays busy, and Baby & Me plus Wonder Works covers all three. We also stopped trying to see it all. Two or three zones, a snack, the slide twice, and out before the afternoon slump is a win with a toddler.

The other locationMADE for Kids, the makerspace

The Magic House runs a second, separate space called MADE for Kids, a hands-on makerspace in the Delmar Loop (about 20 minutes from Kirkwood, not at the main museum). It has its own admission (recently about $6, with a $3 after 3pm deal and free parking), and it leans older: digital painting, a pottery wheel, button-making, 3D printing, robotics and game design, best for school-age kids who can read the prompts. A nice hack: St. Louis Public Library cardholders can check out a free MADE family pass at a branch. Treat it as a separate outing, not a same-day add-on, unless your kids are older and tireless.

Logistics parents ask aboutFood, strollers and the practical bits

A few things worth knowing before you arrive. There is a café on site, the Picnic Basket, with meals and snacks and an online preorder option that saves a queue at lunch. Outside food is allowed only on the outdoor Garden Pavilion, so pack accordingly. For specific accommodations, strollers, a nursing space, bag storage or sensory-friendly options, it is worth a quick call to the museum (314-822-8900) to confirm current details before you go, since these change. And because much of the fun is climbing and building, dress kids in clothes that can take it.

The St. Louis questionMagic House or City Museum?

Families visiting St. Louis often try to choose between the Magic House and the City Museum, and the honest answer is they are different animals. The Magic House is more educational and gentler, with a dedicated baby-and-toddler space, so it wins for younger kids (roughly 1 to 8) and for a calmer day. The City Museum is a wild, artist-built climbing playground that rewards school-age and older kids with energy to burn, and it is more intense. With little ones, start with the Magic House; with big kids, the City Museum is the one they will talk about. Many families with a range of ages simply do both on a St. Louis weekend.

The Magic House sits in downtown Kirkwood, which is a pleasant pairing in itself: the historic Kirkwood Farmers' Market and walkable shops and cafes are close by, and Powder Valley Nature Center (free) is a short drive for a dose of outdoors after all that indoor play. Before you go

How much does the Magic House cost?

All-day admission has recently been about $15 a person for ages 1 and up, with under 1 free and free parking included. Local residents from seven nearby counties pay just $3 a person from 3 to 5pm with ID, and eligible families get in free on Free Family Nights (the third Friday of the month, 5 to 8pm). Prices change, so confirm the current rate before you go.

Is the Magic House free on Fridays?

On one Friday a month. The Magic House holds Free Family Nights on the third Friday of each month from 5 to 8pm, free for up to two adults and four children from eligible counties with proof of residency. Other Fridays are regular admission, though the $3-after-3pm resident deal applies daily.

How long should you spend at the Magic House?

Plan a half to full day. It can easily fill a whole day, but with toddlers and preschoolers, three to four hours before the afternoon slump is the sweet spot. If you only have part of a day, the late-afternoon 3 to 5pm window is both the cheapest for residents and the least crowded.

Is the Magic House in downtown St. Louis?

No. The Magic House is in Kirkwood, a southwest suburb of St. Louis at 516 S. Kirkwood Rd, about 20 to 25 minutes from downtown. It is a common mix-up; the main museum is in Kirkwood, while the separate MADE for Kids makerspace is in the Delmar Loop closer to the city.

What is the best age for the Magic House?

Roughly 1 to 10. There is a dedicated Baby & Me space for the littlest ones, Wonder Works and the Children's Village for preschoolers, and the Make-It Workshop and STEAM Center for school-age kids. It skews a little younger than the City Museum, which suits older, more adventurous kids better.

Magic House or City Museum, which is better for kids?

It depends on age. The Magic House is gentler and more educational, best for younger kids (about 1 to 8) and a calmer day. The City Museum is a wild, artist-built climbing playground best for school-age and older kids with energy to burn. With a range of ages, many families do both on a St. Louis weekend.

Do you have to pay for parking at the Magic House?

No. Parking at the Magic House is free and included with admission, in the museum's own lot. On busy weekend middays the main lot can fill and an overflow lot opens, another reason to go on a weekday or in the late afternoon.
